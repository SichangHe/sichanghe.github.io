# Geometric Measurements of the Axiom of Choice in Neural Proof Embeddings
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `mendozasmith2026geometric`, [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28572)

## What is measured
The paper trains a denoising Transformer only on constructive Lean proofs, embeds tactic sequences, and measures how proofs depending on `Classical.choice` differ. The population is 42,355 LeanDojo-traced theorems with 2 to 200 tactic invocations: 31,144 classical and 11,211 constructive (Section 1, p. 2; Table 16). The encoder trains on 8,968 constructive proofs; the k-NN detector is fit on them and scored on a held-out constructive test split of 1,122 and a fixed random sample of 5,000 classical proofs for the aggregate AUC (Section 2, p. 4). The depth-2 k-NN AUC is 0.847; separation falls to near chance (0.507) at depth 9+. It also evaluates 251 held-out theorems with Lean `aesop` under a 60-second timeout. The authors describe the findings as "a robust association" between Classical.choice dependence, embedding geometry and prover behaviour that does not "prove a unique causal mechanism for the operational gap" (Appendix B). Inference: the results therefore depend on the Lean/Mathlib versions, dependency labels, data filtering, model training, and the timeout harness.

## technique: tactic-head representation and denoising encoder
Represent each proof as the ordered sequence of tactic heads (`intro`, `rw`, `simp`), discarding arguments. The vocabulary has 219 heads; sequences fit a 64-step window (Section 1.1, p. 3; 1.4M parameters, Appendix G). Mask 20% of non-special tokens and train a 4-layer encoder plus 2-layer decoder on constructive proofs only. Mean non-padding hidden states form a 128-dimensional embedding, then L2-normalize to the unit sphere. Quote: “To turn these sequences into a geometry, we train a denoising autoencoder on constructive proofs alone.” Inference: for Verus, tokenize proof blocks or verifier diagnostics, train only on accepted proof traces, and use a `spec fn` only to describe embedding data; no learned score can replace Verus checking. The abstraction loses tactic arguments, so it may miss semantic differences inside the same head.

## technique: one-class anomaly detection
Fit k-NN (k=5) on constructive embeddings and score held-out proofs by mean distance to nearest constructive neighbors. The aggregate detector reaches AUC 0.672 (length-residualized 0.675); the depth-stratified rows use all 31,114 classical embeddings at depth 2 or greater and give depth-2 AUC 0.847 (k-NN), 0.801 (KDE), 0.782 (Isolation Forest), falling to 0.507, 0.495, 0.463 at depth 9+ (Section 2, p. 4, Table 1). KDE (h=0.3 selected by 5-fold CV) and Isolation Forest reproduce the gradient (Table 1). Quote: “Proofs near the constructive cluster receive low scores, while proofs far from it receive high scores.”

## technique: reconstruction-loss measurement
For each proof, draw ten independent masks hiding 20% of non-special tokens and average masked-token cross-entropy. Constructive held-out baseline is 2.47 nats/token; shallow classical d≤2 is 3.32 nats/token, +34.5%, with Mann–Whitney one-sided p≈3×10^-101 (Section 2.1, p. 5). Quote: “The excess attenuates with depth and is at the constructive baseline by depth 9+.” Inference: analogous Verus data could measure predictability of proof-step sequences, but model loss is not a soundness metric and denominators must remain tied to the masking protocol.

## technique: density-superlevel containment
Fit a Gaussian KDE on constructive training embeddings, define S90 as the region containing the densest 90% of held-out constructive proofs (so 10% of constructive proofs fall outside by construction), and report the fraction of classical proofs outside it (Section 2.2, p. 5). At depth 2, 43% lie outside S90; rates are 33% at depth 3, 18% at depths 4–6, 12% at depths 7–8, and 8% at depth 9+. The threshold is calibrated on constructive proofs only, without classical labels. Inference: use this to detect distribution shift in generated Verus proofs, retaining accepted proofs regardless of score.

## technique: anomaly-guided prover triage
Replace each original proof body in 251 theorems with `by aesop`, compile with a 60-second timeout, and mark success only when Lean exits code 0 with no `sorry` or error (Section 4, p. 7; Appendix H.1). Aesop solves 20.0% of constructive and 1.5% of Classical.choice-reached theorems (Fisher odds ratio 16.5); success stays near zero across all four classical depth buckets, unlike the geometric gradient. A 5-fold cross-validated logistic regression predicting aesop failure from log length alone has median bootstrap AUC 0.766; adding the k-NN anomaly score raises it to 0.841, a paired improvement of +0.071 with bootstrap interval [+0.003, +0.179] over 1000 resamples (p. 8). The authors say within-class intervals cover zero at this sample size, so the effect holds at the full-sample level only. The ReProver byT5-small hybrid (top-8 first tactics, each followed by aesop) solves 22.0% of constructive and 4.5% of classical theorems (odds ratio 6.02), so it compresses the gap but does not close it (Table 5). Quote: “Neural guidance doesn’t close the gap.” Inference: Verus could use a learned triage model to choose between invariant templates or allocate proof-search budget; every candidate still requires Verus verification.

## techniques for the catalog
- tactic-head denoising embedding: learn proof-composition geometry from masked constructive tactic sequences
- one-class k-NN anomaly: score distance from a constructive proof population
- reconstruction-loss probe: measure masked-token cross-entropy on held-out proofs
- KDE superlevel containment: flag embeddings outside a calibrated constructive density region
- anomaly-guided triage: combine proof anomaly with length to predict automated prover failure
