# CTL* Verification and Synthesis using Existential Horn Clauses
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `carelli2024ctl`, [paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11502v1)

## scope and claims
The paper translates first-order CTL* verification of infinite-state transition systems with fairness into satisfiability of existential Horn clauses (EHCs), and extends the same encoding to synthesize missing conditions and assignments. The paper is by Mishel Carelli and Orna Grumberg; its first page identifies arXiv:2408.11502v1, dated 21 August 2024. The abstract states: “This work proposes a novel approach for automatic verification and synthesis of infinite-state reactive programs with respect to CT L∗ specifications, based on translation to Existential Horn Clauses (EHCs).” (Abstract, p.1). Its central contract is satisfiability iff the program meets the specification, with soundness and relative-completeness theorems (Introduction, pp.1–3).

## EHC representation and well-foundedness
An EHC implication has a constrained body and an existentially quantified head; a second clause kind requires a relation to be disjunctively well founded. The exact definition is: “Implications are formulas with uninterpreted predicates of the form:” followed by `c(v0) ∧ q1(v1)… → ∃w: b(w0) ∧ p1(w1)…` (Section 2.1, p.4). A `dwf(q)` clause means the interpretation of `q` is contained in a finite union of well-founded relations. Inference: use this representation when verification needs invariant predicates together with existential choices and progress arguments. Construct the clauses using the `Trans` rules below; a `dwf` requirement alone does not imply that a relation is well-founded. Section 2.1 uses the result that a relation is well-founded if its transitive closure is disjunctively well-founded (p.5). The paper explicitly says, “Existential Horn Clauses (EHCs) with their solver E-HSF [3], is an extension of CHCs, which include existential quantification as well as the power of handling well-foundedness.” (Introduction, p.2)

EHCs encode disjunction and negation through fresh Boolean variables and complementary predicates. For `body → head1 ∨ head2`, add `body → ∃a: head(a)`, then `head(0) → head1` and `head(1) → head2`; for negation use `p(v) ∧ q(v) → ⊥` and `⊤ → p(v) ∨ q(v)` (Section 2.1, p.5). This matters in CTL* rules whose heads contain choices. Inference: a possible Verus adaptation would express suitable relations as specification predicates and prove the translated implications. This would require a separate account of temporal semantics and well-foundedness; the paper neither describes nor validates a Verus encoding.

## translating CTL* temporal structure
Apply the eight `Trans` rules recursively until only assertions remain. Rule 1 replaces a basic state subformula by a fresh predicate `aux`. Generate clauses verifying that subformula from initial condition `aux`, and clauses verifying the outer formula with the subformula replaced by `aux`. Thus `aux` underapproximates the satisfying states; negation normal form makes the replacement sound (Section 3, p.7). Rule 2 replaces a non-fair path quantifier with its fair counterpart and removes fairness from the program because the basic formula no longer depends on those conditions (p.7).

For `X c`, add Boolean `xX` and constrain the next-state relation with `xX = c(v′)`. For an existential path, add `init(v) → ∃xX: aux(v,xX)` and verify from `aux` (Rule 3, pp.7–8). For `G c`, add `xG = c(v) ∧ xG′` and fairness condition `xG ∨ ¬c(v)`; for existential `E_f`, initialize `xG` existentially through `aux` (Rule 4, p.8). For `c1 U c2`, use the recurrence `xU = c2(v) ∨ (c1(v) ∧ xU′)` and fairness `¬xU ∨ c2(v)` (Rule 5, pp.8–9). For the existential `U` case, also add `init(v) → ∃xU: aux(v,xU)` and use `aux` as the initial condition. The recurrences and added fairness conditions enforce the temporal meaning of the new variables along fair paths. Rule 8 ends the translation with `init(v) → c(v)` for an assertion `c` (p.10). Inference: a Verus adaptation would need an explicit path model and proofs connecting these Boolean variables to temporal formulas, beyond their local recurrences.

## universal and existential fairness rules
For `A_f c`, Rule 6 introduces predicate `p` for states reachable from bad initials, predicate `t` containing the transitive closure of `next`, relation `r` connecting states through fairness events, and `dwf(r)`. The paper explains: “The new predicate p(v) represents states that are reachable from initial states that do not satisfy c(v).” (Rule 6 explanation, p.9). Add closure clauses for `p` and `t`. When fairness conditions are present, require `r` to contain pairs connected through a sequence visiting each fairness condition in order. An infinite fair path from a bad initial state would yield an infinite transitive chain within `r`, contradicting `dwf(r)` (Section 3.1, p.12). With no fairness conditions, require `p(v) ∧ t(v,v′) → r(v,v′)`, so `r` includes all positive-length reachability pairs starting in `p`, rather than only single transitions (Rule 6, p.9). For `E_f c`, Rule 7 requires each initial state to satisfy `c` and `q_1`. Each `q_i` must have a successor that either advances cyclically to `q_(i%|J|+1)` when `J_i` holds in the current state, or remains in `q_i` along `r_i`. The clauses require each `r_i` to be both transitive and disjunctively well-founded, hence well-founded; this forces eventual progress to `J_i`. The paper states, “The new predicate qi represents the states from which it is possible to get to a state s that satisfies Ji.” (Rule 7 explanation, p.10). With no fairness set, require every initial state to satisfy `c` and have a successor in `q` forever.

Inference: a possible proof adaptation would use rankings for progress toward fairness events and an invariant tracking the current event index. The paper does not establish that the required relations admit rankings expressible in Verus.

## synthesis from partial programs
For a sketch containing holes in conditions or assignments, replace each hole with an uninterpreted predicate and use the condition predicate and its complement for the two branches, preserving the data variables. Model an assignment hole by a relation `ua(vr,vr′)` and add `⊤ → ∃vr′: ua(vr,vr′)` for every valuation of `vr`. This requires a total relation, permitting nondeterministic multiple assignments rather than requiring a function (Section 5, pp.16–17). Then run the same CTL* translation and ask E-HSF for an interpretation. The paper summarizes the procedure: “We obtain this by first filling each hole with an uninterpreted predicate. Additional constraints are added to guarantee that predicates associated with a condition or an assignment indeed behave accordingly.” (Introduction, p.3). Inference: use this when the desired behavior is a CTL* formula and the implementation shape is fixed but guards or updates are unknown. The solver interpretation is the synthesized filling; soundness means the filled program satisfies the formula and relative completeness means that a realizable synthesis problem produces satisfiable clauses under a semantic interpretation (Theorems 4–5, Section 5.1, p.17). This does not guarantee a filling expressible in a fixed first-order syntax: “However, this interpretation might not be expressible by a first-order formula in our logic.” (Section 3.2, p.13).

Inference: a separate synthesis engine could propose Rust guards or updates for subsequent verification. An arbitrary relational filling still needs to be turned into executable code, and verification must establish the intended temporal obligations. Candidate generation alone would not establish correctness.

## techniques for the catalog
- EHC encoding: represent constrained invariant implications and disjunctively well-founded relations, then solve with E-HSF
- CTL* elimination: recursively replace `X`, `G`, and `U` with Boolean monitor variables and fairness constraints
- fairness proof: refute bad universal paths with a well-founded relation, or construct cyclic existential paths with ranked progress predicates
- hole synthesis: model missing guards and assignments as uninterpreted predicates with complementary condition branches and total, possibly nondeterministic assignment relations, then solve the translated EHC system
