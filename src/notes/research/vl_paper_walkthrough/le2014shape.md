# Shape Analysis via Second-Order Bi-Abduction
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `le2014shape`, [paper](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/430f66f4683c7b67138a4cb9b053671c646e1b01) (link from the fetch record; the paper text gives no DOI; it appears as CAV 2014, LNCS 8559, pp. 52–68)

## central method

The paper synthesizes separation-logic heap predicates while verifying each method. Its abstract states: “We present a new modular shape analysis that can synthesize heap memory specification on a per method basis.” (abstract, page 52). Unknown second-order predicates stand for pre/post shapes; verification generates relational assumptions, then derivation and normalization turn those assumptions into definitions. The method is implemented as S2 inside HIP and targets memory safety, which the paper describes as safety “from null dereferencing and memory leaks” (section 7, page 63).

## technique: second-order bi-abductive entailment

Use this when a method's heap shape is unknown and fixed list predicates are too restrictive.

1. put fresh unknown predicates in the method contract, for example `requires H(x,q)` and `ensures G(x,q)` (section 3, page 55). The paper distinguishes unknown pre-predicates (which may be strengthened) from unknown post-predicates (which may be weakened) (abstract, page 52).
2. run ordinary Hoare-style symbolic verification, but replace each heap entailment with the extended procedure (section 1, page 53; Hoare rules in Fig. 6, page 60).
3. given antecedent Δa and consequent Δc, infer a frame residue Δf and relational assumptions R such that `R ∧ Δa` entails `Δc * Δf` (section 3, pages 54–55).
4. when an unknown predicate in the antecedent meets a points-to assertion (or known predicate) in the consequent, unfold it into the node plus fresh unknown predicates for each field and for a remainder predicate (Fig. 3, page 58). When an unknown occurs in the consequent, fold the reachable heap pieces into it, stopping at `#` parameters (Fig. 4, page 59).
5. accumulate R through the method body and calls; once the body and the postcondition check are done, `solve(R)` = `PRED_NORM(PRED_SYN(R))` produces the definitions, and later methods reuse them (rule SA-METH, Fig. 6, and text on page 60).

The paper summarizes the purpose: “The core of the new entailment procedure generates a set of relational assumptions on unknown predicates to ensure memory safety.” (section 1, page 53). Its list-to-doubly-list example (`sll2dll`) generates four assumptions (section 3, pages 55–56, Fig. 2): A1 establishes `G` in the null branch; A2 unfolds `H` to a node for the field access `x->prev`; A3 establishes the precondition of the recursive call, with a heap guard; A4 establishes `G` as the postcondition after the call.

Inference: this paper does not target Verus. A Verus front end could infer missing heap facts and emit `requires` clauses, ghost ownership predicates, and `proof fn` lemmas; a verified Rust function would still need those generated contracts checked by Verus. The separation-logic frame is an inference layer, not a direct Verus construct.

## technique: #-annotations and heap guards

Use this for predicates with back pointers or multiple parameters, where unrestricted unfolding can instantiate one location repeatedly. Parameters are either unannotated (instantiatable) or marked `#` (non-instantiatable). During unfolding, each unannotated parameter is instantiated at most once and `#` parameters are never instantiated; during folding, heap traversal starts from the unannotated parameters and stops at `#` parameters (section 4, pages 57–58). This avoids formulas such as `U3(y,y)` or `U2(r,y)*U3(y,x#)`, which “may cause a trivial FALSE pre-condition to be inferred” (section 4, page 57). The annotation is automatic: pre-predicate parameters that are not field-accessed in the method body get `#`, and post-predicate parameters stay unannotated (section 4, page 57). For `sll2dll`, `q` is not field-accessed, giving `requires H(x, q#) ensures G(x, q)`.

When folding, the procedure also records, as a heap guard, known heaps reachable from the `#` parameters (section 4, page 58). The paper says: “Such heap guards help with capturing the relations of heap structures and recovering those relationships when necessary (e.g. back-pointer x#).” (section 4, page 58). In the `sll2dll` example the guard `x→node(q, xn)` in A3 “is crucial for predicate derivation” (section 3, page 56).

Inference: represent a Verus analogue with ghost references or maps and explicit separation/disjointness predicates. State the non-duplication condition in `requires`; preserve it in loop `invariant` declarations and prove fold/unfold lemmas with `proof fn`. Verus ownership may supply some alias exclusion, but the paper's automatic #-inference is not a Verus feature.

## technique: derive predicates from relational assumptions

Use this after verification has generated R. The algorithm `PRED_SYN` (Fig. 7, page 60) first exhaustively applies base splitting (`[syn-base]`, section 5.1, page 61), which separates assumptions mixing a pre-predicate and a post-predicate. It then repeatedly picks one unknown and assumption. For pre-predicates, it applies case splitting to make guards disjoint (`[syn-case]`), groups the disjoint cases (`[syn-group-pre]`), derives a definition (`[syn-pre-def]`), inlines it into remaining assumptions, and discharges obligations (sections 5.2, pages 61–62). For post-predicates, it groups the assumptions and defines the post-predicate as their disjunction (`[syn-group-post]`, `[syn-post-def]`; section 5.3, page 62). Post-predicates are derived only after all pre-predicates have been derived (section 5.3, page 62). The paper says pre-predicate definitions are obtained “without any weakening, thus ensuring soundness of our pre-conditions” (section 5.2, page 62).

The paper's `sll2dll` result before normalization is `H(x,q) ≡ emp ∧ x=NULL ∨ x↦node(xp,xn) * Hp(xp,q) * H(xn,x)` and `G(x,q) ≡ emp ∧ x=NULL ∨ x↦node(q,xn) * G(xn,x)` (section 3, page 56). The base case is the empty heap at null; the recursive case owns the current node and recurses on the next field (and, for `H`, also carries a dangling-candidate `Hp` for the prev field).

Inference: a Verus implementation could generate candidate `spec fn` predicates over ghost sequences/maps and then emit proof lemmas whose `ensures` establish each relational assumption. `spec fn` definitions describe logical shapes; executable functions must not call `proof fn` at runtime. The paper's solver and separation heap are not automatically available in Verus.

## technique: normalize synthesized shapes

Use normalization to make inferred contracts smaller and reusable. `PRED_NORM` has four steps: (i) eliminate dangling predicates, (ii) eliminate useless parameters, (iii) re-use predicate definitions, and (iv) perform predicate splitting (section 5.4, pages 62–63). Step (i) replaces undefined dangling predicates by special variables, which helps keep the link between pre- and post-conditions; step (ii) drops arguments unused in the definitions, using second-order entailment; step (iii) uses an equivalence proof to match a new definition with one previously provided or inferred; step (iv) splits predicates into smaller ones to increase reuse (pages 62–63). In the list example it removes an unused `q` parameter (giving `H2(x)`) and turns the dangling `prev` field into the marker `Dp` (section 3, page 56).

The paper's soundness theorem states: “If Δa Δc ; (R, Δf ) and Γ=PRED NORM(PRED SYN(R)) then Γ : ΔaΔc ∗ Δf .” (theorem 6.1, page 63; the extracted text loses the entailment symbols). Lemma 4 (page 63) says that if `PRED_NORM` returns a solution Γ' from a solution Γ then `R(Γ')` holds, and the paper argues that “normalization is meaning preserving” (section 6, page 63). The proofs are in the technical report [21], not in this paper.

Inference: in Verus, normalization can simplify ghost predicate arguments and factor reusable `spec fn`s, but each rewrite needs a proof lemma preserving the original `ensures`. A generated contract should be rechecked after normalization; textual equivalence is not enough.

## evidence and measured scope

S2 was evaluated on lists, cyclic lists, doubly linked lists, skip lists, trees, rose trees, mcf trees and tree/list combinations such as `tll` (section 7, page 63). Table 1 reports predicate size, synthesis time (each without and with normalization) and re-verification time in seconds for 32 examples on an Intel i7-960 (3.2GHz) with 16 GB of RAM (section 7, pages 63–64). The paper states: “In total, the specification inference took 8.37s while the re-verification took 8.25s.” (section 7, page 64). Normalization is stated to reduce “by 68% (169/533) the size of synthesized predicates with a time overhead of 27% (8.37s/10.62s)” (section 7, page 65). Reading the numbers against Table 1: 533 is the sum of the `w/o norm.` size column and 8.37 s the sum of the `w/o norm.` Syn. column; 10.62 s is the sum of the `w/ norm.` Syn. column. The `w/ norm.` size column as printed sums to 166, not 169 (this agent's own addition of the extracted table; the printed `swl` verification time “22” looks like a typo for 0.22, which makes the 8.25 s total work). Re-verification is optional: “Due to our use of sound inference mechanisms, re-verification is not strictly required.” (footnote 4, page 64). These figures are the paper's aggregate over its listed examples, not a general performance guarantee.

On four Glib files, S2 inferred memory-safe specifications for 192/216 procedures and loops (89%; Fig. 8, page 65). The files were `gslist.c` (698 LOC, 33 procedures, 18 loops, 47 inferred), `glist.c` (784, 35, 19, 49), `gtree.c` (1204, 36, 14, 44), and `gnode.c` (1128, 37, 27, 52). Adding the printed per-file counts gives 141 procedures + 78 loops = 219, not 216 (this agent's addition; the 192 matches). Footnote 5 (page 65) says array data structures are not supported, “Hence, some procedures like g tree insert internal cannot be verified.”

The stated limitation is direct: “Our present proposal cannot handle graphs and overlaid data structures since our instantiation mechanism always expands into tree-like data structures with back pointers.” (section 7, page 65). The paper claims soundness depends on the entailment procedure (conclusion, page 67), with proofs left to the report [21]; the authors nevertheless re-verified inferred specifications to illustrate integration with a verifier (footnote 4). The CIL translation is another assumption: “Our translation preserves the semantics of source programs, subject to CIL's limitations.” (footnote 3, page 63).

Inference: for agent-written verified Rust, this technique threatens less the final checker than the specification layer. It could infer ownership-shape contracts for recursive lists and trees, reducing manual boilerplate, but graphs, alias-heavy unsafe code, arrays, and domain-functional properties remain outside the demonstrated coverage (the paper itself says memory safety only, and lists richer specifications as future work, section 7, page 65). A wrong inferred shape can make a contract too weak or too strong; Verus must check the emitted contract and code, and external memory/hardware behavior stays trusted unless separately modelled.

## techniques for the catalog

- second-order bi-abduction — infer relational heap assumptions and frame residues while verifying with unknown predicates
- guarded fold/unfold — use #-annotated parameters and heap guards to control aliases and preserve back-pointer context
- relational predicate synthesis — derive disjoint preconditions and disjunctive postconditions from collected assumptions
- predicate normalization — eliminate dangling structure and unused parameters, reuse equivalent definitions, and split predicates
- modular shape inference — synthesize per-method memory-safety contracts and compose them through verified calls
