# VerifyThis 2019: A Program Verification Competition (Extended Report)(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)Tag: `dross2020verifythis`, [paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13610v3)## scope and resultThe report describes a two-day, human-centred competition: 13 teams, 21 participants, and three 90-minute verification challenges. The paper says, "VerifyThis aims to bring together researchers and practitioners interested in formal verification, providing them with an opportunity for engaging, hands-on, and fun discussion" (section 1, p. 2). Its evidence is therefore both technical (algorithms, specifications, proofs) and empirical (submissions, interviews, and a regression over 21 historical challenges).## technique 1: staged specification and invariant strengthening for monotonic segmentswhat it is and when to use itChallenge 1A asks for an array scan that returns maximal monotonic cutpoints, then asks for progressively stronger properties. The paper defines four base properties: non-empty, begin-to-end, within bounds, and monotonic; maximality is an advanced strengthening. The authors give the algorithm as a single scan: maintain `x` and `y`, determine whether the current segment is increasing, advance while the relation remains unchanged, append `y`, then continue. The paper’s exact algorithm description is: "maximal monotonic cutpoints can be computed by scanning s once while storing every index that corresponds to a change in monotonicity (from increasing to decreasing, or vice versa)" (section 2.1.1, p. 4).how to apply it1. Represent the input sequence and cutpoints in the verifier’s convenient model; the organizers explicitly allow arrays, mathematical sequences, or dynamic lists (section 2.2, p. 5)2. Prove non-empty, endpoint, and bounds properties for the result (section 2.1.1, p. 4)3. Prove that every interval between adjacent cutpoints is increasing or nonincreasing (section 2.1.1, p. 4)4. Strengthen the predicate with maximality and prove that extending a segment at either endpoint breaks monotonicity; teams found it preferable to state right and left maximality separately (section 2.4, p. 7)5. Add loop invariants relating processed indices, the current segment direction, and already emitted cutpoints; the report says teams that succeeded needed “a few key invariants” (section 2.3, p. 6)Verus form
