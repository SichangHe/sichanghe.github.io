# Baldur: Whole-Proof Generation and Repair with Large Language Models
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `first2023baldur`, [paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3611643.3616243)

whole-proof generation: fine-tuned llm emits complete isabelle proof, isabelle checks it
- avoids per-step model-prover interaction of search
- input: theorem statement; target: full human proof; sample 16 or 64 candidates, any accepted counts
  - reject `sorry` and `oops`, 10 s timeout per step
- minerva 8B and 62B on 6,336 pisa test theorems
  - 8B: 34.8% (16 samples), 40.7% (64)
  - 62B with context: 47.9% (64)
- 64 samples cost about 35 s on tpuv3 vs 216 s for thor search, excluding cpu checking
error-conditioned repair
- input: theorem, failed proof, first isabelle error; output: new complete proof
- train on failures of the generator on training theorems paired with human proofs
- needs the error message: repair without it did not beat generation at equal cost
- one generate + one repair adds 4.2% (266) but costs two samples; controlled gain is 1.5%
- limit: only learns first-error types; prefix-based data is future work
theory-file context
- prefix up to 50 preceding statements from the same file, left-truncated to 1,536 tokens
- 8B at 64 samples 40.7% → 47.5%
  - of 5 inspected successes, 3 copied nearby proofs
repeated repair and ensembles
- generate, repair, repair again: 24.9% at cost 2, 26.2% with second repair vs 25.4% generation alone at cost 3
- 10-model ensemble 58.0%; union with thor (57.0% alone) 65.7%
caveats
- theorem-wise split and possible minerva pretraining leakage limit claims to new projects
- percentages use different denominators; topic counts overlap
verus takeaways (inference)
- same loop: generate proof body, run verus, keep only verified candidates without `assume`
- repair prompt: rejected proof plus verbatim verus diagnostic
  - diagnostics may be less localized than isabelle's
- needs verus corpus and project-wise holdout
