# Transformational Verification of Quicksort
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `deangelis2020transformational`, [paper](https://doi.org/10.4204/EPTCS.320.7)

The paper (VPT/HCVS 2020, EPTCS 320, pp. 95–109) verifies a functional Scala Quicksort by compiling contracts to constrained Horn clauses (CHCs), eliminating list terms, and solving the resulting Boolean and linear-integer clauses. It says: “We verify these properties by: (1) translating them into CHCs, (2) transforming the CHCs by removing all list occurrences, and (3) checking the satisfiability of the transformed CHCs by using the Eldarica solver over booleans and integers.” (abstract, p.95). The target is the pre/postcondition contracts of `partition`, `quicksort` and `append`. The paper also notes that “our specific Quicksort verification problem is not solved by STAINLESS” (Section 1, p.96).

## technique: contract-to-chc verification
Use this when recursive code manipulates lists and a solver cannot perform induction over list structure. First write executable functions and `require`/`ensures` contracts (in the paper: pure Scala with `require`/`ensuring`). Translate each function pattern match to CHC clauses: constructors become list terms, guards become linear-integer constraints (natural numbers become non-negative integers), and the returned Boolean becomes an argument. For `partition`, the paper gives clauses for `all_grt`, `all_leq`, and `partition`, then turns the postcondition into two false-headed goals: “false :- B=false, partition(X,L,L1,L2), all_grt(X,L1,B).” (G1) and the corresponding `all_leq` goal G2 (Listing 3, p.98). The `B=false` constraint is used “to avoid negative literals in the body of goals, and hence to stick to Horn format” (p.98). For Quicksort, the contracts become goals G3 to G7 (Listing 8, pp.101–102): G3 and G4 (`all_grt`/`all_leq` preserved by `quicksort`), G5 (`isSorted`), G6 (`count`), and G7 (the `append` contract). Satisfiability of the program clauses plus a goal means that contract holds.

Inference: In Verus, executable Rust would remain ordinary `fn` code over `Seq` or `Vec`; the contract would be `requires`/`ensures`, and a failed postcondition would become a proof obligation rather than a false-headed CHC. The CHC translation itself does not carry over, but the separation of implementation, contract, and solver-checkable obligations does.

## technique: fold/unfold list elimination
Apply this after CHC generation when lists block induction. The paper's Elimination Algorithm uses the definition, unfolding, and folding rules. Introduce a predicate folding the goal, as the paper does with `pl(A,B) :- partition(B,C,D,E), all_leq(B,E,A)` (clause 1). Unfold the definition and recursive calls; remove clauses whose constraints are unsatisfiable; fold recursive clauses back through the new predicate. For the example, unfolding clause 1 with respect to `partition` produces clauses 2–4, unfolding clauses 2 and 4 with respect to `all_leq` yields clauses 5–7, clause 6 is removed because it has an unsatisfiable constraint, clause 7 is equal to clause 3 modulo equivalence of constraints and is removed, and folding clause 3 with clause 1 gives `pl(A,B) :- A=true, B>=0` and `pl(A,B) :- B>=0, pl(A,B)` (Section 2, pp.98–99). The final goal is `false :- A=false, pl(A,B)`; the paper says Eldarica “easily finds that pl(A,B) :- A=true, B>=0 is a model” (p.99). For `partition` the transformed set is satisfiable iff the original is; for the Quicksort algorithm Rcata guarantees only one direction: if TG is satisfiable, the original set is satisfiable (Section 4, p.102).

## technique: staged lemma reuse (Rcata)
The Quicksort extension is the list-removal strategy Rcata (Section 4, pp.102–104). It loops over Define-Fold, Unfold, and a new Replacecata step that adds atoms from a set Props of implications, which are contracts already proved at earlier stages. For G5 (sortedness), Rcata starts with `qss(A) :- quicksort(B,C), isSorted(0,C,A)`. Props from G1 and G2 (partition postcondition) add `all_grt(B,D,true)` and `all_leq(B,E,true)`; G3 and G4 add `all_grt(B,F,true)` and `all_leq(B,G,true)` (clauses 4–5). The paper says G6 and G7 are not needed for G5. To fold the recursive `quicksort` atoms, the paper adds `isSorted` atoms whose output is an unbound variable; this is justified by totality of `isSorted`. The G7 lemma cannot be used there because it needs those outputs bound to `true`. The second iteration folds twice (clause 7), drops the `partition`/`all_grt`/`all_leq` conjunction using totality of `partition` and G1/G2 (clause 8), and introduces `a(B,X,Y,Z,T,U,B1,B2,A)` (clause 9) to fold clause 8 into clause 10: `qss(A) :- B>=0, qss(B1), qss(B2), a(B,0,0,0,true,true,B1,B2,A)`. Clause 9 still has list atoms, so Rcata continues; the list-free result is in the Appendix (p.109). The paper states that the lemma use is why the algorithm needed extending: “during one stage of the transformation we will use as lemmas some contracts that we have verified at previous stages” (Section 1, p.96).

The specification functions (`all_grt`, `all_leq`, `count`, `isSorted`) are “parameterized catamorphisms”: list recursion with an extra parameter, so `isSorted(0,l)` expresses sortedness without splitting the `Cons` case (Section 3, p.100). The paper argues this form helps pick the new predicates (“eureka definitions”) in Section 5.

Inference: A Verus analogue is to prove small lemmas about `Seq` operations first, then invoke them in a recursive Quicksort proof. Verus `lemma` functions are proof-time constructs; use `assert`, `requires`, `ensures`, and `invariant` on loops, not CHC predicates. The paper's Boolean/LIA model omits Rust ownership, aliasing, and machine overflow unless those are modeled explicitly.

## evidence, trust, and limits
The evidence is satisfiability of the transformed clauses by Eldarica. Only the set TG5 for G5 is printed in full (Appendix, p.109): “Both Eldarica and Z3 are able to prove the satisfiability of this set of clauses.” For the other goals the paper only says: “Similarly, by applying again the List Removal strategy and then proving satisfiability by Eldarica over LIA and Bool, we are able to verify all contracts of the Quicksort program.” (Section 4, p.104). No benchmark denominator or runtime table is reported. The paper also reports that STAINLESS “is able to verify the contracts of the functions partition and append, but not the one of quicksort” (Section 4, p.104). The paper says the strategy is not fully automatic: it leaves a fully automatic Rcata, and discovery of the lemmas (`all_grt`, `all_leq`) when the programmer does not supply them, “for future research” (Section 5, p.105). The proof “depends critically on the contract specifications” (p.105).

Inference: Trusted components are the Scala-to-CHC encoding (done by hand in the paper as far as the text shows), the soundness of the transformation, and the solver. The totality of the functions is used as a premise in the transformation, so the method assumes it. The method assumes pure functional pattern matching and contracts expressible in Bool and LIA through catamorphisms. The paper does not claim termination or resource-bound proofs.

Inference: For VeruLaw, the technique is a medium threat and opportunity. It suggests automatic preprocessing for recursive list proofs, but a product emitting verified Rust still needs ownership-aware specifications and a trusted Verus kernel. Existing work means VL should not redo generic fold/unfold theory; it could adapt staged lemma discovery to Verus sequences.

## techniques for the catalog

- contract-to-chc: translate recursive functions and postconditions into CHCs plus false-headed violation goals
- fold/unfold elimination: remove inductive list terms by unfolding, simplifying constraints, and folding recursive predicates
- staged catamorphism lemmas: reuse previously proved contracts while eliminating data structures from later goals
