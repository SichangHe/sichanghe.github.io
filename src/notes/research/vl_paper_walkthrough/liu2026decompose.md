# Decompose-and-Formalise: Recursively Verifiable Natural Language Inference
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `liu2026decompose`, [paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19605v1)

## scope and result

The paper introduces LLM-TP Tree, a neuro-symbolic pipeline that turns natural-language entailment explanations into an entailment tree, decomposes sentences into atomic propositions, autoformalises them, and verifies the tree bottom-up with a theorem prover. Its abstract describes the three core operations: “decomposes premise-hypothesis pairs into an entailment tree of atomic steps,” “verifies the tree bottom-up to isolate failures to specific nodes,” and performs “local diagnostic-guided refinement instead of regenerating the whole explanation” (Abstract, p. 1). The system uses Isabelle/HOL axioms and lemmas plus LLM-generated translations; it is not a Verus or Rust implementation.

Experiments use FOLIO, ProofWriter, PrOntoQA, and EntailmentBank with five LLM backbones (section 4, p. 5). Table 1 reports final verified percentages for each dataset/model; for example, Qwen3-Max on EntailmentBank reaches 78.67% (Table 1, p. 6). Averaged over backbones, LLM-TP Tree improves final verified rate over Explanation-Refiner by 26.23, 21.73, 21.60, and 48.93 percentage points on FOLIO, ProofWriter, PrOntoQA, and EntailmentBank respectively (section 5, p. 6). Table 2 gives average refinement iterations of 2.88 for LLM-TP Tree versus 4.02 for Explanation-Refiner, across five backbones and all four datasets (Table 2, p. 6). These are benchmark results, not guarantees that the natural-language meaning was captured.

## technique 1: entailment-tree construction and recursive obligations

What it is. Given premises P and hypothesis h, prompt an LLM to construct a tree of premise and intermediate-conclusion nodes such that selected premises plus conclusions entail h. For every internal node v, the local obligation is that the conjunction of descendants entails the parent: `∧u∈D(v) φu |= φv` (section 3.1, p. 3). The chain is recursively verifiable when each intermediate step is individually solver-checkable and the final hypothesis is proved.

How to apply it.

1. Ask the model for a tree and intermediate conclusions, optionally selecting a subset of premises, with `P' ∪ C |= h` as the target (section 3.1, p. 3).
2. Represent every node with its natural-language proposition and descendant links.
3. Turn each subtree into local proof obligations. Explanation leaves become axioms; the hypothesis node gets a lemma requiring the conjunction of explanation atoms to entail each hypothesis atom (section 3.4, pp. 4–5).
4. Verify bottom-up. Once a root is certified, promote it as support for its parent; continue until the final hypothesis is certified (section 3.4, p. 5).
5. If a lemma fails, retain the prover's failed goal and relevant constraints and localize the failure to implicated explanation nodes (section 3.4, p. 5).

Verus form. Inference: encode each tree node as a predicate or ghost proposition. A child-to-parent entailment becomes a `proof fn` with `requires` equal to child facts and `ensures` equal to the parent fact. Verified subtrees can be exposed as proved lemmas to parent functions. Verus checks the encoded relation; it does not infer a tree from prose or validate that the chosen predicates preserve natural-language meaning.

Evidence and limits. The paper explicitly warns that its guarantee is conditional on the translation function Φ, target formalism, and theorem prover (Limitations, p. 8). A formally proved node can still be semantically wrong if Φ changes scope, polarity, or roles. Inference: VL must treat autoformalisation as a specification boundary and review the mapping before trusting a Verus proof.

## technique 2: entailment-preserving atomic decomposition

What it is. Complex premises, intermediate conclusions, and hypotheses are split into shorter atomic propositions before autoformalisation. For a sentence φ and atom a in D(φ), the decomposition must satisfy `φ ⊨ a`, so it cannot introduce unsupported information (section 3.2, p. 3). Candidate atoms are generated by an LLM and filtered by a pretrained RoBERTa-large-MNLI classifier at threshold 0.9 (section 3.2, p. 3).

How to apply it.

1. Construct the entailment tree first; identify propositions that participate in proof obligations.
2. Prompt an LLM to produce atomic propositions for each such sentence.
3. Run the NLI classifier on each atom against its source sentence and retain atoms whose entailment score meets 0.9 (section 3.2, p. 3).
4. Translate only the retained, semantically focused atoms into logical forms.
5. Keep the source-to-atom relation so a failed formalisation can be traced to the original sentence.

Verus form. Inference: represent each atom as a separate `spec fn` predicate and prove a lemma that the source-level contract implies the atom-level contract. Use `assert` to expose each atom before invoking downstream lemmas. The 0.9 classifier threshold is not a Verus proof; in a VL pipeline it should be replaced or supplemented by a compile-checked implication where feasible.

Evidence and limits. The ablation without atomic decomposition increases average refinement iterations by 0.31–0.53 relative to full LLM-TP Tree (section 5, p. 6). Table 2's full system averages 2.88 iterations versus 3.31 without atomic decomposition, across four datasets (Table 2, p. 6). The classifier can miss commonsense or discourse entailments, and the paper's limitation says naturalistic NLI may require additional axioms or richer semantics (Limitations, p. 8).

## technique 3: θ-substitution autoformalisation

What it is. Instead of generating one monolithic logical formula, start with an abstract event-based template and apply substitutions that preserve argument-role bindings. The paper defines θ as a mapping from placeholders to terms and composes entity, event, and semantic-role substitutions: `φfinal = φ0[θ1 ◦ θ2 ◦ θ3]` (section 3.3, p. 4).

How to apply it.

1. Create an abstract first-order template with generic predicates and variables.
2. Apply entity substitution θ1 to map predicates to named entities.
3. Apply event substitution θ2 to introduce event predicates and event structure.
4. Apply role substitution θ3 to add agents, patients, and other arguments consistently.
5. Compose the substitutions and send the final formula to the theorem prover. The paper's forest-fire example maps ForestFire, Deer, and Woodland, then adds Die/Leave events and Agent/Patient roles (Example 3.1, pp. 4–5).

Verus form. Inference: use ghost structs or enums for entities and events, with `spec fn` relations such as `agent(e, x)` and `patient(e, y)`. Write separate proof lemmas for each substitution invariant, then prove the final contract from those invariants. Verus has no direct θ-substitution operator; the mapping is a code-generation or ghost-proof layer. The semantic-role predicates and their intended meaning remain assumptions unless axiomatized and proved.

Evidence and limits. Table 3 measures faithfulness by converting logical forms back to natural language and computing cosine similarity. Averaged over five backbones and four datasets, LLM-TP Tree scores 0.893 versus 0.760 for Explanation-Refiner and 0.840 for Faithful-Refiner; removing θ-substitution lowers the full system by an average of 0.034 (Table 3, p. 7; section 5, p. 7). Cosine similarity is a proxy, not a semantic proof. The paper reports that naturalistic discourse, coreference, modality, and temporality can remain unrepresented (Limitations, p. 8).

## technique 4: diagnostic-guided local refinement

What it is. When a theorem prover cannot discharge a local lemma for atom a, extract diagnostics (failed goal and relevant constraints), identify a small implicated node set, and prompt the LLM to refine only those nodes. Recheck the same local obligations; do not regenerate the entire explanation. The abstract calls this “local diagnostic-guided refinement” (Abstract, p. 1).

How to apply it.

1. Verify every subtree bottom-up.
2. On failure, capture the prover's goal and constraints and map them to explanation nodes.
3. Regenerate or edit only the implicated node statements or formalisation.
4. Re-run the local subtree obligations.
5. Promote the subtree when all atoms are certified; repeat at the parent level (section 3.4, p. 5).

Verus form. Inference: map a failed Verus obligation to the proof function or lemma whose `requires`/`ensures` is unsatisfied, then regenerate only that lemma's intermediate assertions or contract. Keep unaffected modules and verified lemmas intact. Verus compiler diagnostics are more concrete than an LLM's generic critique, but localization still depends on mapping diagnostics to source nodes.

Evidence and limits. The method's average 2.88 iterations beats Explanation-Refiner's 4.02 and Faithful-Refiner's 3.51 (Table 2, p. 6). It also maintains logical reasoning accuracy; Figure 5 reports the strongest neuro-symbolic accuracy among compared systems on FOLIO, ProofWriter, and PrOntoQA (section 5.2, p. 7). Local repair can preserve a wrong global abstraction if the prover accepts an incorrectly translated node; the paper's conditional guarantee and semantic-misalignment limitation remain.

## what is proved, measured, and trusted

The theorem prover checks induced Isabelle/HOL formulas and produces solver-checkable certificates. The experiments measure final verified explanation rates, iteration counts, runtime, and cosine faithfulness; they do not prove that Φ preserves every natural-language commitment. Trust includes LLM tree construction, atom generation, the 0.9 RoBERTa entailment filter, θ-substitution templates, event semantics, Isabelle/HOL, and the benchmark labels. The paper itself says a guarantee is conditional on “the chosen autoformalisation function Φ, the target formalism, and the capabilities of the underlying theorem prover” (Limitations, p. 8).

Inference: the threat to agent-written verified Rust is medium to high. The tree, atomic obligations, and local diagnostic repair are directly useful for breaking a large Verus proof into lemmas and repairing one failed obligation. Semantic translation errors can still produce a provable but wrong contract, and background facts may be omitted or encoded as unproved axioms. VL should reuse the decomposition and repair architecture while requiring Verus-checked contracts and explicit trust records for every external lemma. The paper's NLI datasets and cosine metric are already sufficient for their domain; VL should not redo them as evidence of Rust correctness.

## techniques for the catalog

- recursive entailment tree — represent multi-step reasoning as bottom-up local obligations and promote verified subtrees
- entailment-preserving atoms — split sentences into atoms only when each is entailed by its source, using a 0.9 NLI filter
- θ-substitution formalisation — instantiate entity, event, and semantic-role placeholders in separate, auditable steps
- diagnostic-guided local repair — use failed prover goals to refine only implicated nodes and recheck the same subtree
