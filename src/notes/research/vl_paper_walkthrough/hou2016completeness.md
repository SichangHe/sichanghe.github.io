# Completeness for a First-order Abstract Separation Logic
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `hou2016completeness`, [paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.06729v1)

## result
Hou and Tiu define first-order abstract separation logic (FOASL), a labelled sequent calculus `LSFOASL`, and a theorem prover. FOASL weakens concrete points-to: an abstract points-to term denotes the heap returned by a total function and need not be a singleton. This relaxation makes a sound and complete calculus possible while theories approximate concrete models. The abstract states: “We prove that FOASL is sound and complete with respect to an abstract semantics, of which the standard SL semantics is an instance.”

## technique 1: abstract the heap, retain separation structure
Use a separation algebra `(H, ◦, ϵ)` satisfying identity, commutativity, associativity, cancellativity, indivisible unit, disjointness, and cross-split. Interpret an n-ary abstract points-to predicate with a total function `f_n : D^n → H`; formula `t1 ↦ …, tk` is true exactly when `f_n` returns the current world. Keep separation conjunction `*`, magic wand `−*`, first-order quantification, and modality `♦`. The semantics table defines `A * B` by splitting the current world and `A −* B` by all compatible extensions (Section 2, pp. 4–5).

To apply it, choose a concrete or abstract model, define `f_n`, then reason with labelled worlds. Reynolds heaps are an instance when `H` is finite partial maps and `f2(a,b)` is the singleton heap `[a ↦ b]` (Section 2, p. 6). The abstraction is deliberate: “we do not assume that the constructed heap from a tuple is a singleton heap” (Section 1, p. 4).

Verus form (Inference): Verus’s ownership and `Map`/sequence specifications can model a heap fragment and separating resources as ghost predicates, but Verus does not provide FOASL’s `*` or magic wand. One would encode disjointness and frame facts as `spec fn` predicates and prove split/merge lemmas with `requires` and `ensures`; this is an encoding, not a native logic translation.

## technique 2: labelled sequent proof search
Represent a sequent as `G; Γ ⊢ Δ`, where `G` contains relational atoms `(h1,h2 ⊲ h3)` meaning `h1 ◦ h2 = h3`, and Γ/Δ contain labelled formulas. Start a proof of `F` from `⊢ h : F` and apply rules backwards. Logical rules split `*`, introduce `−*`, instantiate `∃`, and move between worlds for `♦`; equality rules replace equal terms or labels. Two points-to rules express totality and injectivity. The paper states: “These rules allow one to replace a term (label) with its equal anywhere in the sequent.” (Section 3, p. 7).

Use structural rules E, D, A, P, C, and CS to enforce separation algebra laws (symmetry, unit, associativity, partial determinism, cancellativity, and cross-split). The system is sound: if `⊢ h : F` is derivable, `F` is valid (Theorem 1, Section 3, p. 8). For an implementation, replace the unrestricted equality rules with substitution-oriented `=′2` and `∼′2`, then prove closure under term and label substitutions (Lemmas 6–7, Section 6, p. 14).

Verus form (Inference): each labelled formula can be a ghost proposition over a resource state; relational atoms become lemmas describing map disjoint-union. Backward proof search itself is an external tactic or generated Rust proof, while individual rule soundness can be encoded as Verus `proof fn` lemmas with `requires`/`ensures`. Do not write `proof fn` calls into executable code.

## technique 3: completeness by Hintikka saturation and countermodel
For an unprovable formula, enumerate a fair schedule of all rule instances and construct an increasing sequence of finite sequents. Add the appropriate premise or fresh label/term whenever its scheduled instance applies. Take the union as the limit sequent. A Hintikka sequent must satisfy 26 closure conditions, including connective decomposition, quantifier witnesses, modality worlds, equality substitution, and all separation-algebra closure properties (Section 4, pp. 8–11).

Construct a countermodel from the limit sequent:

1. take closed terms as a Herbrand domain
2. quotient labels by the equivalence relation generated by `h ∼ h'`
3. use label classes as worlds; define `◦` from relational atoms and `ϵ` as `[ϵ]`
4. define each abstract points-to function from labelled points-to facts in Γ
5. interpret ordinary predicates from labelled atomic facts
6. prove by induction on formulas that Γ is true and Δ false

The paper’s key statement is: “The completeness proof is done via a counter-model construction similar to that in [20,16] for the propositional case, but our proof is significantly more complicated.” (Section 1, p. 4). Hintikka’s Lemma gives falsifiability; Theorem 2 concludes that every FOASL-valid formula is derivable (Section 4, pp. 10–12). This is a meta-theoretic completeness result, not a claim that proof search terminates on every input.

Verus form (Inference): a finite instance can be represented with ghost sets of labels and an explicit equivalence relation, then proved closed under each rule. The infinite fair schedule and quotient model are outside ordinary executable Rust; use them as a specification-level argument or a separate trusted meta-theory.

## technique 4: theories approximate concrete separation logics
Instead of adding a new inference rule for each concrete points-to property, add formulas as assumptions. For Reynolds’s semantics the paper gives six theories: empty-heap exclusion, non-composability with two nonempty heaps, distinct addresses for separating points-to, injectivity, existence of a splittable extension, and abstract-to-concrete implication. It proves these formulas valid in Reynolds’s model and derives most LSSL points-to rules (Lemma 4–5, Section 5.1, pp. 12–13). The two-premise HC rule remains unsupported.

Add theory 7, `∀e1,e2. ♦(e1 ↦ e2)`, for Vafeiadis–Parkinson total addressability. Add theory 8 to enforce Lee et al.’s compatibility behavior. For acyclic-list semantics, define a `path` predicate and add `path(e1,e2) → e1 ≠ e2`; the authors warn that without inductive definitions this cannot force a least fixed point (Sections 5.2–5.4, pp. 13–14).

Verus form (Inference): encode each chosen theory as a `spec fn` or `axiom`-like trusted interface only when its model has been separately justified; prove concrete map properties as lemmas. A theory assumption is trusted specification input, not evidence that arbitrary Rust heaps satisfy it.

## implementation evidence and limits
The prover extends an earlier Reynolds-SL prover, adds predicates and modalities, and builds in linked-list/tree rules. Table 2 lists 12 formulae: formulas 1–4 take `<0.001s`, formula 5 takes `0.9s`, formula 6 `<0.001s`, formula 7 `0.0015s`, formula 8–10 `<0.001s`, formula 11 `<0.001s`, and formula 12 `0.0025s` (Section 6, pp. 14–15). These are selected formulas, not a benchmark denominator or completeness-performance study. The implementation omits HC from proof search. It also trusts the added theories, parser, search implementation, and underlying host language.

What is proved: soundness and completeness for FOASL semantics; admissibility and validity of the listed theory rules; selected automated derivations. What stays trusted: model choice, theory assumptions, implementation, and any translation from Rust ownership to separation algebra. Contradiction check: the paper calls formula 7 “0.0015s” in Table 2, while the surrounding prose only says the prover can handle examples; no denominator is supplied, so no general speed ratio is justified.

Threat to VeruLaw (Inference): FOASL’s useful idea is modular heap reasoning by a small core calculus plus model-specific theories. This could inform a Verus library of reusable disjoint-map and frame lemmas. It does not threaten Verus’s ownership soundness directly because FOASL abstracts heaps and omits the Rust borrow model; adopting its theory assumptions without proving correspondence would enlarge VL’s trusted base. VL should not redo a generic completeness proof unless it targets a Rust-specific resource logic and provides a mechanized connection.

## techniques for the catalog
- abstract points-to semantics — replace singleton heaps with total heap-valued functions to regain completeness
- labelled separation calculus — reason about worlds, splits, magic wand, quantifiers, and modalities with sequents
- Hintikka countermodel construction — fairly saturate unprovable sequents and build a falsifying quotient model
- model theories as assumptions — approximate concrete points-to behavior by formulas instead of new proof rules
