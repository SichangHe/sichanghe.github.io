# Towards Concurrent Quantitative Separation Logic
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `fesefeldt2022towards`, [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02822v3) (arXiv id from the paper text: arXiv:2207.02822v3, 30 Sep 2024)

## scope
This paper combines separation logic for heaps, concurrent ownership, and probabilistic weakest-preexpectation reasoning. Its target is a lower bound on the probability that a concurrent pointer program reaches a postcondition, including programs that may not terminate almost surely. The authors state: “In this paper, we develop a novel verification technique to reason about programs featuring concurrency, pointers and randomization.” (Abstract, p. 1). The result is a guarded command language (chpGCL), Markov Decision Process semantics, weakest resource-safe liberal preexpectations (wrlp), and sound backward proof rules.

## technique: model concurrent probabilistic heap programs
Use chpGCL for assignments, probabilistic choice `{C}[ep]{C}`, sequencing, conditionals, loops, atomic regions, parallel composition, allocation, disposal, lookup, and mutation, plus `diverge` and the terminated program (Def. 3.1, p. 6). The MDP rules are in Figs. 2–4 (pp. 7–8). A state is a program plus stack and finite heap; an MDP supplies nondeterministic actions for thread interleavings and probabilities for random commands. Atomic regions must be tame: no allocation or concurrency, although probabilistic choice is allowed (Sec. 3, p. 5); such programs are called tame. The paper explains the reason: “The reason to restrict the program fragment within atomic regions is that non-tame statements introduce non-determinism (as addresses to be allocated and schedulings of concurrent programs are chosen non-deterministically)” (Sec. 3, p. 5). Schedulers are deterministic and may use history; the paper says this “especially rules out any randomised scheduler” (Sec. 3, p. 8).

How to apply it: (1) write the heap-manipulating program in the grammar; (2) identify each stack/heap state and every enabled interleaving action; (3) give each probabilistic command its distribution; (4) model the resulting transitions as an MDP, with abort transitions for lookup, mutation or disposal of an unallocated address (Def. 3.3 and Figs. 2–4, pp. 6–8); (5) select a qualitative resource invariant describing every shared heap fragment reachable between thread steps.

Verus form. Inference: executable Rust can represent stack variables, `Box`/owned heap cells, and thread state, while ghost predicates describe ownership and disjoint heap regions. Verus is not discussed in the paper, and I did not check Verus documentation; assuming it has no MDP or probabilistic expectation primitive, probabilities and scheduler choices therefore belong in ghost/spec mathematics or an external model, with Rust proofs checking each transition's safety conditions.

## technique: weakest resource-safe liberal preexpectation
QSL expectations map stack/heap pairs to nonnegative values rather than booleans. The paper defines `wrlpⁿ⟦C⟧(X | ξ)` recursively for a postexpectation `X` and resource invariant `ξ`, then takes the limit over `n` steps (Def. 4.1, Sec. 4, p. 9). For zero steps it is one; a terminated command (with n ≠ 0) yields `X`; otherwise it is `ξ −⋆ step(λC′. wrlpⁿ⁻¹⟦C′⟧(X | ξ) ⋆ ξ)`, i.e. one-step transitions taken with any heap satisfying `ξ` added, with `ξ` required to be re-established afterwards. For the invariant `[emp]` wrlp and wlp coincide (Theorem B.10, Appendix B; Sec. 4, p. 10), allowing lower bounds on probabilities of qualitative postconditions (Sec. 4, p. 10).

Apply it backward: choose a postexpectation `Y`, a candidate lower bound `X`, and `ξ`; prove `X ≤ wrlp⟦C⟧(Y | ξ)` by rules. Keep `ξ` covering all shared-memory states between inter-thread inferences; it may be violated during executions of atomic regions (Sec. 4, p. 10). The paper's guess for `ξ` in the running example was made “by collecting all possible values stored in location r during executions yielding our postexpectation” (Example 4.2, p. 13). The conservative-extension theorem states: “For a framing-enabled non-probabilistic program C and qualitative expectations φ, ψ and ξ, we have φ ≤ wrlpJCK (ψ | ξ) iff ξ |= {φ} C {ψ}.” (Theorem 4.1, p. 10).

Verus form. Inference: encode `ξ`, `X`, and `Y` as ghost predicates/spec functions and prove the inequality as lemmas over an explicit transition relation. Use `requires` for the resource invariant and `ensures` for preservation; use loop `invariant` clauses for bounded approximations. The expectation algebra and limit remain an inferred mathematical layer, not a Verus library feature.

## technique: sound proof-rule calculus
The basic rules (Fig. 5, p. 11) handle termination, assignment, lookup, mutation, allocation, and disposal; they reuse the sequential QSL rules and only lower-bound the preexpectation. Control-flow rules (Fig. 6, p. 11) compose sequence, conditionals, loops, divergence, probabilistic choice, atomic regions, sharing, and concurrency. The concurrency rule (`concur`) concludes `X1 ⋆ X2 ≤ wrlp⟦C1 ∥ C2⟧(Y1 ⋆ Y2 | ξ)` and requires `Write(Ci) ∩ Vars(C3−i, Y3−i, ξ) = ∅`: each thread's written variables must not occur in the other thread's program, its postexpectation, or `ξ`. The paper says only local or read-only variables are used and that shared stack variables owned by the invariant are left out “for the sake of brevity” (Sec. 4, p. 11). Auxiliary rules (Fig. 7, p. 12) are superlinearity, wlp-wrlp, frame, atom, monotonicity, max, min, and convex. Min requires a precise `ξ` and a non-probabilistic `C`; convex requires a precise `ξ` and `Write(C) ∩ Vars(e) = ∅`. Superlinearity is stated for wlp only, needs `C` almost surely terminating with respect to the scheduler set, and the paper says it requires a restricted scheduler set “to enforce fairness conditions” (p. 11–12). The paper proves: “For every proof rule in Figures 5–7 it holds that if their premises hold, the conclusion holds as well.” (Theorem 4.2, p. 12).

Step-by-step use: (1) annotate each command with pre/post expectations and `ξ`; (2) read the annotation bottom-to-top; (3) apply basic rules to atomic commands; (4) use sequence and branch rules; (5) supply a loop invariant; (6) split concurrent postexpectations and discharge write-set side conditions; (7) use share (with `ξ = [emp]`, `π` the old invariant) to move the invariant into the pre- and postexpectation; (8) use wlp-wrlp and monotonicity to obtain the desired probability bound (Sec. 4, pp. 11–13). Example 4.2 notes that proofs in this annotation style “should only be read backwards from bottom to top” and omit applications of the atom and share rules (p. 12).

The worked two-thread example initializes `r=-1`, randomly sets `r` to 0 or 1 with probability 0.5, and repeatedly reads it. The authors choose `ξ = max{[r↦0],[r↦−1]}` and establish `0.5 ≤ wrlp⟦C⟧([y=0] | ξ)`; with share, monotonicity and wlp-wrlp this gives `0.5 ⋆ ξ ≤ wlp⟦C⟧([y=0])` (Example 4.2, pp. 12–13; details Appendix F.1). Inference: this pattern maps to Verus ghost ownership plus a loop invariant connecting the read variable to permitted heap values; each probability claim needs an independently formalized expectation model.

## producer-consumer case, evidence, and trust
Section 5 applies the rules to a producer, a consumer and a lossy channel (Fig. 8, p. 14). The producer randomly generates 1 or 2 with probability 0.5 into an array of size k; the channel copies each entry to a second array with probability p and otherwise stores −1; the consumer counts valid entries in `l`. With `ξJ` for a location set J, the paper obtains `([0 ≤ k] · p^|{0..k}∩J| · (1−p)^|{0..k}\J|) ⋆ ξJ ≤ wlp⟦C⟧([l = |J|] ⋆ ξJ)` (Sec. 5, p. 15). The authors say that with a resource invariant for a data structure the example “can be used as a template to reason about systems communicating using a shared data structure” (p. 13). The calculations are in Appendix F.2; the “at least half transmitted” bound uses superlinearity and relies on the program being almost surely terminating under a fairness condition, which the paper asserts but does not prove (Sec. 5 p. 14; App. F.2). No benchmark count, runtime percentage, or denominator is reported: the evidence is soundness theorems and worked derivations, not an implementation evaluation.

Trusted elements are the MDP operational semantics, expectation algebra, resource-invariant side conditions, and the proof of every rule in Appendix E. Inference: the threat to a product that emits agent-written verified Rust is medium for concurrency and high for quantitative probability: the ownership decomposition is directly useful, but scheduler fairness, random sampling, and expectation limits are outside ordinary Verus checking. The paper's own stated limits: only lower bounds are verified “due to the concurrent rule” and probabilistic resource invariants are left open (Sec. 6, p. 15). Inference: VL should reuse the ownership/resource-invariant discipline rather than reimplement the logic without a concrete probabilistic requirement.

## techniques for the catalog
- chpGCL-to-MDP semantics — model heap commands, probabilistic choices, and nondeterministic thread interleavings as an MDP
- resource-safe liberal preexpectation — bound postcondition probability while framing a shared heap invariant and allowing nontermination
- backward wrlp proof rules — compose assignment, heap, control-flow, atomic, probabilistic, and concurrent rules bottom-to-top
- quantitative frame and share — split local heap resources and enrich or discharge the resource invariant
- precise-invariant side conditions — min and convex rules require a precise resource invariant (for any stack at most one heap with nonzero value)
