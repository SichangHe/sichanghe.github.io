# A Domain-Specific Harness for End-to-End Automation of Optimization Research
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)

## harness and trust boundary
AutoOPT joins numerical optimization, LLM symbolic discovery, Lean verification, and human interpretation. Its four stages are numerical design with BnB-PEP, analytic discovery of an algorithm and convergence proof, formal verification in Lean, and human write-up (Figure 1, p. 2). The authors define a domain-specific harness as scaffolding that augments a general LLM with expert methodology; “the human researcher” approves each stage (Section 1, pp. 2–3). The paper's two case studies are lemniscate acceleration and analytic ITEM-f, whose convergence theorems are machine-checked in Lean 4 (abstract, p. 1).

The workflow stores goals, sources, claims, evidence, experiments, and next steps in a versioned research workspace. It begins with a structured interview rather than guessing missing mathematical details, and writes code only after the user approves the derivation (Section 3.1, pp. 9–10). The authors explicitly caution that a formalisation can prove a weaker theorem than intended or remain incomplete with `sorry` (Section 3.4, p. 14).

## technique: BnB-PEP numerical design and certificates
Use BnB-PEP when searching fixed-step first-order methods for a function class with a worst-case measure. The paper's procedure is: (1) specify the class, performance measure, initial condition, and horizon N; (2) parameterize each iterate by a lower-triangular stepsize array; (3) reduce the infinite-dimensional function maximization to sampled values, gradients, and interpolation inequalities; (4) lift inner products into a positive-semidefinite Gram matrix; (5) solve the resulting SDP for fixed stepsizes; (6) dualize it, obtaining nonnegative multipliers and a PSD slack matrix as a convergence certificate (Sections 3.1, pp. 7–9). A feasible dual point proves an upper bound by weak duality; the paper calls it “an explicit nonnegative aggregation of the sampled inequalities proving that E ≤ νr for every function in the class satisfying the initial condition” (Section 3.1, p. 9).

(7) Parameterize the PSD slack as `Z = V Vᵀ`, yielding a nonconvex QCQP over algorithm coefficients and proof variables; (8) solve with a fixed-method SDP, local interior-point refinement, and optional spatial branch-and-bound for global certification (Section 3.1, pp. 9–10). The generated JuMP program records derivation, code, solver status, numerical solution, and logs. The paper warns that interpolation exactness and dimension assumptions are required; the Gram rank constraint is dropped only when dimension is at least the number of trajectory columns (Section 3.1, pp. 7–9).

Inference: for Verus, a domain harness could produce candidate algorithm coefficients and a symbolic certificate offline, then encode the final recurrence and bound as executable Rust plus `requires`/`ensures`. Verus does not provide SDP or QCQP solving; numerical certificates remain trusted inputs until independently reconstructed as `proof fn` lemmas. Use `spec fn` for mathematical functions and loop `invariant` clauses for each iteration, with no claim that the solver can infer the PEP certificate.

## technique: numerical-to-symbolic discovery
After Stage 1, `frontier-llm-consult` receives a small context bundle containing the sweep, derivation, and relevant mathematics. The paper's three substeps are: (1) fit an analytic parametrization of stepsizes and a feasible dual point, checking formulas against recorded horizons; (2) recast the lower-triangular method into a memory-efficient recursive or momentum form; (3) read dual multipliers to construct a Lyapunov sequence whose one-step decrement is a nonnegative combination of interpolation inequalities (Section 3.2, pp. 11–12). The output is explicitly a candidate that must be tested and formalized. The paper says, “Every proposed formula is a candidate, not a proof” (Figure 4 prompt, p. 12).

Inference: the same separation is useful for Verus: keep model-generated formulas in a candidate file, test them numerically, then prove the recurrence and Lyapunov decrease in small `proof fn`s. A Rust agent should report which coefficient identities are assumptions and which are proved. The paper's ChatGPT-specific consultation route does not transfer as a Verus feature.

## technique: independent formal verification gate
Stage 3 formalizes candidate definitions, theorem statements, and Lyapunov proofs in Lean. Its steps are: (1) make every definition and proof step explicit; (2) run Lean's kernel; (3) run an independent comparator that rechecks the public theorem surface and permitted assumptions; (4) ask human authors to confirm that formal statements match the manuscript (Section 3.3, pp. 13–14). The authors state: “A proof is recorded as formally verified only after two successive independent checks followed by a final human confirmation.” (Section 3.3, p. 13)

For lemniscate acceleration, the project has 10 public declarations, 22 dependency nodes, 22 source files, and 7,384 Lean lines. It builds with Lean 4.32.0/mathlib 4.32.0, has 8,687 native build jobs, and comparator replay times of 109 seconds on macOS and 155 seconds in isolated WSL2; all public declarations use exactly `propext`, `Quot.sound`, and `Classical.choice` (Table 2, pp. 26–27). The project passes a hygiene scan with no `sorry`, `axiom`, `admit`, or `unsafe` (Section 4.1.5, pp. 26–27). ITEM-f has 14 public declarations, 38 files, 7,985 lines, 8,694 build jobs, and comparator replay times of 105 and 115 seconds; it reports the same three standard axioms and no unfinished proof placeholders (Section 4.2.4, pp. 34–35).

Inference: in Verus, mirror the gate with a pinned toolchain, a clean verification build, an audit rejecting `external_body` in code under verification unless explicitly documented, and an independent replay of public contracts. Verus's constructs are `requires`, `ensures`, `spec fn`, `proof fn`, `assert`, and loop `invariant`; do not write Lean's `lemma` keyword or treat `invariant(...)` as a call. A second checker cannot establish that the contract captures product intent, so human review remains necessary.

## technique: human interpretation and evidence labels
Stage 4 chooses an implementable formulation and labels each claim as human-verified, formally verified, LLM-verified, numerical evidence, or conjecture (Section 3.4, p. 14). The authors recommend publication restraint and extracting generalizable insights from opaque machine-discovered algorithms. They also state: “Even with thorough harnesses, AI systems still make mistakes, and the human authors are ultimately accountable for the work they publish.” (Section 3.4, p. 14)

## what is proved, trusted, and relevant to VL
The paper proves two optimization convergence theorems in Lean and reports numerical BnB-PEP searches. The formal projects prove the statements encoded in Lean, not every natural-language claim; the self-H-duality remark is explicitly recorded without detailed proof or Lean verification (Section 4.3, p. 36). Trusted elements include Lean kernel and mathlib, the comparator implementation, solver correctness and tolerances, interpolation theorems, model outputs, and the human choice of theorem statement. Inference: threat to a product built from agent-written verified Rust is high at the specification and numerical-certificate boundaries, but lower for algebraic proof steps that are independently checked. VL should reuse the staged candidate/check/replay pattern and evidence labels; it should not redo generic LLM discovery or PEP infrastructure. The decisive adaptation is measuring whether the same independent contract replay catches a Rust implementation that satisfies a weakened specification.

## techniques for the catalog
- PEP-to-SDP reduction — reduce worst-case first-order optimization to finite interpolation inequalities and a Gram-matrix SDP
- dual certificate search — optimize algorithm coefficients jointly with nonnegative multipliers and PSD proof slack
- numerical-to-symbolic recasting — fit formulas, derive memory-efficient updates, and turn multipliers into Lyapunov decrements
- candidate labeling — mark model formulas as candidates until independently checked
- two-check formal gate — run the proof assistant, an independent comparator, and final human statement review
- evidence-status reporting — distinguish formal, human, LLM, numerical, and conjectural claims
