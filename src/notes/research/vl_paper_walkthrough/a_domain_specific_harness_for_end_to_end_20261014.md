# A Domain-Specific Harness for End-to-End Automation of Optimization Research
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `kim2026domain`, [paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07407v1)

## harness and trust boundary
AutoOPT joins numerical optimization, LLM symbolic discovery, Lean verification, and human interpretation. Its four stages are numerical design with BnB-PEP, analytic discovery of an algorithm and convergence proof, formal verification in Lean, and human write-up (Figure 1, p. 2). The authors define a domain-specific harness as scaffolding that augments a general LLM with expert methodology (Section 1.2, p. 3). They specify that “every stage is approved or revised by a human” (Section 2, p. 6). The paper's two case studies are lemniscate acceleration and analytic ITEM-f, whose convergence theorems are machine-checked in Lean 4 (abstract, p. 1).

The workflow stores goals, sources, claims, evidence, experiments, and next steps in a versioned research workspace. It begins with a structured interview rather than guessing missing mathematical details, and writes code only after the user approves the derivation (Section 3, pp. 7, 10). The authors explicitly caution that a formalisation can prove a weaker theorem than intended or remain incomplete with `sorry` (Section 3.4, p. 14).

## technique: BnB-PEP numerical design and certificates
AutoOPT uses BnB-PEP to search fixed-step first-order methods for a specified function class and worst-case performance measure. The paper's procedure is: (1) specify the class, performance measure, initial condition, and horizon N; (2) parameterize each iterate by a lower-triangular stepsize array; (3) reduce the infinite-dimensional function maximization to sampled values, gradients, and interpolation inequalities; (4) lift inner products into a positive-semidefinite Gram matrix; (5) solve the resulting SDP for fixed stepsizes; (6) dualize it, obtaining nonnegative multipliers and a PSD slack matrix as a convergence certificate (Section 3.1, pp. 7–9). A feasible dual point proves an upper bound by weak duality; the paper calls it “an explicit nonnegative aggregation of the sampled inequalities proving that E ≤ νr for every function in the class satisfying the initial condition” (Section 3.1, p. 9).

(7) Parameterize the PSD slack as `Z = V Vᵀ`, with V lower triangular and nonnegative on its diagonal, and introduce auxiliary variables for higher-degree products as necessary to obtain a nonconvex QCQP over algorithm coefficients and proof variables; (8) solve with a fixed-method dual SDP, local interior-point refinement, and spatial branch-and-bound for global certification only on explicit user request (Section 3.1, pp. 9–10). AutoOPT generates Julia/JuMP code and stores the derivation, code, solver status, numerical solution, and logs. A lossless reduction requires interpolation exactness for the chosen function class and dimension at least the number of trajectory columns before dropping the Gram rank constraint. Equality between the inner SDP and its dual additionally assumes strong duality; a feasible dual certificate needs only weak duality (Section 3.1, pp. 8–9). For both case studies, the design formulation uses dimension d ≥ N + 2 (Appendices A.3 and B.3, pp. 50, 65).

Inference: for Verus, a domain harness could produce candidate algorithm coefficients and a symbolic certificate offline, then encode the final recurrence and bound as executable Rust plus `requires`/`ensures`. The optimization solver would supply candidates; `proof fn` lemmas would need to establish their certificate identities and assumptions before the Rust proof could rely on them. Use `spec fn` for mathematical functions and loop `invariant` clauses for each iteration, with no claim that the solver can infer the PEP certificate.

## technique: numerical-to-symbolic discovery
For the external consultation route used by both case studies, `frontier-llm-consult` assembles a small context bundle containing the numerical findings, derivation, and relevant mathematics. The user approves or edits it before it is shared with the frontier model (Section 3.2, pp. 11–12). The paper's three substeps are: (1) fit an analytic parametrization of stepsizes and a feasible dual point, checking formulas against recorded horizons; (2) recast the lower-triangular method into a memory-efficient recursive or momentum form; (3) read dual multipliers to construct a Lyapunov sequence whose one-step decrement is a nonnegative combination of interpolation inequalities (Section 3.2, pp. 11–12). The output is explicitly a candidate that must be tested and formalized. The paper says, “Every proposed formula is a candidate, not a proof; report which entries of the sweep you checked it against and to what precision.” (Figure 4 prompt, p. 12).

Inference: the same separation is useful for Verus: keep model-generated formulas in a candidate file, test them numerically, then prove the recurrence and Lyapunov decrease in small `proof fn`s. A Rust agent should report which coefficient identities are assumptions and which are proved. The paper's ChatGPT-specific consultation route does not transfer as a Verus feature.

## technique: independent formal verification prerequisite check
Stage 3 formalizes candidate definitions, theorem statements, and Lyapunov proofs in Lean. Its steps are: (1) make every definition and proof step explicit; (2) run Lean's kernel; (3) run an independent comparator that rechecks the public theorem surface and permitted assumptions; (4) ask human authors to confirm that formal statements match the manuscript (Section 3.3, p. 13). The authors state: “A proof is recorded as formally verified only after two successive independent checks followed by a final human confirmation.” (Section 3.3, p. 13)

For lemniscate acceleration, the project has 10 public declarations, 22 dependency nodes, 22 source files, and 7,384 Lean lines. It builds with Lean 4.32.0/mathlib 4.32.0, has 8,687 native build jobs, and comparator replay times of 109 seconds on macOS and 155 seconds in isolated WSL2; all public declarations use exactly `propext`, `Quot.sound`, and `Classical.choice` (Table 2, pp. 26–27). The project passes a hygiene scan with no `sorry`, `axiom`, `admit`, or `unsafe` in the development. Its comparator wrappers both import the proof development itself (Section 4.1.5, pp. 27–28). ITEM-f has 14 public declarations, 20 dependency nodes, 38 source files, 7,985 lines, 8,694 build jobs, and comparator replay times of 105 seconds on macOS and 115 seconds in isolated WSL2. It reports the same three standard axioms. Its challenge wrapper imports a proof-free specification layer and contains one approved placeholder per declaration; the solution closes all placeholders. The hygiene scan reports no `sorry`, `axiom`, `admit`, or `unsafe` in the proof development or solution wrapper (Section 4.2.4, Table 3 and Figure 11, pp. 34–36).

Inference: in Verus, mirror these prerequisite checks with a pinned toolchain, a clean verification build, an audit rejecting `external_body` in code under verification unless explicitly documented, and an independent replay of public contracts. Verus's constructs are `requires`, `ensures`, `spec fn`, `proof fn`, `assert`, and loop `invariant`; do not write Lean's `lemma` keyword or treat `invariant(...)` as a call. A second checker cannot establish that the contract captures product intent, so human review remains necessary.

## technique: human interpretation and evidence labels
Stage 4 interprets the results, chooses how to present the available formulations, and labels each claim as human-verified, formally verified, LLM-verified, numerical evidence, or conjecture (Section 3.4, p. 14). The authors recommend publication restraint and extracting generalizable insights from opaque machine-discovered algorithms. They also state: “Even with thorough harnesses, AI systems still make mistakes, and the human authors are ultimately accountable for the work they publish.” (Section 3.4, p. 14)

## techniques for the catalog
- PEP-to-SDP reduction — reduce worst-case first-order optimization to finite interpolation inequalities and a Gram-matrix SDP
- dual certificate search — optimize algorithm coefficients jointly with nonnegative multipliers and PSD proof slack
- numerical-to-symbolic recasting — fit formulas, derive memory-efficient updates, and turn multipliers into Lyapunov decrements
- candidate labeling — mark model formulas as candidates until independently checked
- two-check formal verification — run the proof assistant, an independent comparator, and final human statement review
- evidence-status reporting — distinguish formal, human, LLM, numerical, and conjectural claims
