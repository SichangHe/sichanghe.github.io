# Interpolation synthesis for quadratic polynomial inequalities and combination with EUF(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)Tag: `gan2016interpolation`, [paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.04802v3)## problem and resultThe paper generates reverse Craig interpolants for contradictory conjunctions of concave quadratic polynomial inequalities, including strict inequalities, and combines the method with equality over uninterpreted functions (EUF). A concave quadratic has degree at most two and a negative semidefinite quadratic matrix (Definition 4, Section 3, p. 5). Variables are split into common `x`, A-local `y`, and B-local `z`; the interpolant may use only `x`.The abstract says: “An algorithm for generating interpolants for formulas which are conjunctions of quadratic polynomial inequalities (both strict and nonstrict) is proposed.” Its key observation is that concave quadratic inequalities can be linearized, while a generalized Motzkin transposition theorem supplies the certificate. The algorithm uses semidefinite programming (SDP), recursively eliminating variables when a nonpositive combination yields a sum of squares (SOS).## technique 1: NSOSC base case and SDP certificateNSOSC means that no nonpositive constant combination of the nonstrict input polynomials is a nonzero SOS. When NSOSC holds, generalized Motzkin produces nonnegative multipliers and an SOS polynomial satisfying an identity. The SOS splits into `h1(x,y)+h2(x,z)`; the common-variable expression on the A side is an interpolant.How to apply it.1. Partition contradictory formulas into nonstrict `f_i ≥ 0` and strict `g_j > 0`, with A-local, B-local, and common variables (Problem 1, Section 4, p. 8)2. Check NSOSC by an SDP for nonnegative multipliers `δ_i` and SOS `h` with `Σ δ_i f_i + h ≡ 0` (Algorithm 2, lines 2–3, p. 18)3. In the NSOSC case, solve the SDP certificate for `λ_i, η_j, η_0` and positive semidefinite matrices representing SOS terms (Section 4.2, pp. 12–13)4. Decompose the quadratic SOS `h(x,y,z)` into SOS `h1(x,y)` and `h2(x,z)`; absence of `y_i z_j` cross terms is the required condition (Lemma 4, p. 11)5. Form `I = Σ_{A} λ_i f_i + Σ_{A} η_j g_j + η_0 + h1`. If the strict-side multiplier sum is positive, use `I>0`; otherwise use `I≥0` (Theorem 4, p. 12)6. Verify the numerical SDP result by reconstructing the SOS decomposition; if verification fails, return unknown rather than an unproved interpolant (Remark 1, Section 4.2, p. 13)The paper's certificate is explicit: “there exist λi ≥ 0 … and two quadratic SOS polynomial h1 ∈ R[x, y] and h2 ∈ R[x, z]” satisfying identity (21) and normalization (22) (Theorem 4, p. 12).Evidence. Theorem 4 proves soundness and existence under NSOSC. Theorem 8 states that IGFCH computes an interpolant in the NSOSC case (Section 4.4, p. 18). The paper gives no benchmark denominator in this theorem; SDP complexity is abstracted as `g`.Verus form.
