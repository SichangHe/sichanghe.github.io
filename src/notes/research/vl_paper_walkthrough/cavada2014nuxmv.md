# The nuXmv Symbolic Model Checker
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)
Tag: `cavada2014nuxmv`, [paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-08867-9_22)

## scope and result
Cavada et al. describe nuXmv as a re-engineered symbolic model checker for finite- and infinite-state synchronous fair transition systems. It extends NuSMV with AIGER input, interpolation, k-induction, IC3 variants, LTL k-liveness, integer and real data, SMT model checking, abstraction refinement, model simplification, and tool interfaces. The abstract says: “This paper describes the NU X MV symbolic model checker for finite- and infinite-state synchronous transition systems.” (Abstract, PDF p. 1). It also states that “For infinite-state systems, it extends the N U SMV language with new data types, namely Integers and Reals, and it provides advanced SMT-based model checking techniques.” (Abstract, PDF p. 1)

## finite-state verification portfolio
Use the finite-state flow when the design is Boolean, bit-vector encoded, or supplied in AIGER. Read the AIGER file, populate nuXmv’s internal structures, select an invariant or LTL algorithm, and inspect a generated trace if a property fails (Section 2.1, printed p. 335; Section 3, printed p. 337). The portfolio includes McMillan interpolation, interpolation sequences, k-induction, and IC3. The paper explicitly says: “NU X MV implements a vast portfolio of algorithms for invariant checking.” (Section 2.1, printed p. 335)

Interpolation requires extending MiniSAT to emit a resolution proof; an API then extracts and manipulates interpolants. IC3 with abstraction refinement has two variants, one refining through IC3 and one refining through BMC. Temporal decomposition and equivalence discovery can benefit all these algorithms (Section 2.1, printed p. 335). Inference: a user should choose interpolation when proof-producing SAT refutations are useful, k-induction when a short inductive depth suffices, and IC3 when clause generalization is effective; those selection rules are guidance, not claims made by the paper.

Guided BDD reachability accepts hints written in a restricted PSL SERE fragment; the hints guide reachability and can compute the full reachable set (Section 2.1, printed p. 335). Inference: for a Verus proof, such hints resemble externally supplied candidate invariants, but Verus has no PSL-hint interface in the local reference.

## LTL and liveness
For LTL, nuXmv retains SAT-based BMC and adds k-liveness inside an IC3 framework. k-liveness counts and bounds how many times a fairness constraint can become true, and an LTL monitor is built with LTL2SMV (Section 2.1, printed p. 335). Inference: this is appropriate for fairness-sensitive temporal properties, but a Verus loop proof would need an explicit progress variant or temporal-state model; the paper gives no Verus encoding.

## infinite-state SMT techniques
To model infinite-state systems, declare unbounded integers and reals in the extended language and encode transitions and invariants as SMT formulas. The paper’s example declares a real input `d`, a finite `state`, a real `res`, an initial constraint `res >= 0.0`, a transition constraint bounding `d` by the state, and `INVARSPEC res <= 0.3` (Figure 1, printed p. 336). Lifted approaches include SBMC, k-liveness plus IC3, BMC, k-induction, interpolation, interpolation sequences, and IC3 for infinite-state systems (Section 2.2, printed pp. 336–337). The authors caution that these approaches are “in general incomplete,” although a lasso-shaped counterexample is eventually found and some designs can be proved (Section 2.2, printed p. 336).

Abstraction refinement avoids explicit quantifier elimination in one family: encode the abstract model-checking problem directly as SMT. Classical CEGAR remains available and computes a quantifier-free formula equivalent to the abstract transition relation for selected predicates; this requires AllSAT. nuXmv implements BDD+SMT AllSMT enumeration and a structure-aware partition into smaller abstraction problems. To discard a spurious counterexample, it offers unsat-core, interpolant, and weakest-precondition refinement (Section 2.2, printed pp. 336–337). Inference: a Verus pipeline could use these as candidate predicate or lemma generators, but the final proof still has to establish the exact Rust contract.

## model reduction and architecture
Static analysis extracts possible variable values and re-encodes them, such as storing a two-valued 32-bit word with one Boolean; constant and free-input propagation further simplify the model (Section 2.3, printed p. 337). nuXmv removes NuSMV’s limit of 64-bit vectors (Section 2.3, printed p. 337). Its architecture retains NuSMV symbol tables, flattening, Boolean encodings, CUDD BDDs, and MiniSAT, adds a resolution-proof builder and interpolant API, and exposes a MathSAT5 interface for satisfiability, AllSMT, unsat cores, interpolants, and quantifier elimination (Section 3, printed pp. 337–338). IC3 engines can run as a library or external executable through AIGER, with violated-property traces converted back to nuXmv traces (Section 3, printed p. 337).

## evidence and limitations
The paper compares plots on HWMCC13 finite safety and liveness tracks and reports that current nuXmv solves more safety problems than its competition submission, iimc, and v3, while ABC remains top in safety. In liveness, iimc remains top and nuXmv outperforms v3 and tip (Section 4, printed p. 338). The SMT plots use bit-vector benchmarks from earlier IC3 work, SV-COMP 2013, and InvGen, plus Lustre programs for LIA; the paper says nuXmv solves more plotted instances than the compared tools, but the plots do not provide a single tabulated denominator (Section 4, printed pp. 338–339). Applications include Rockwell-Collins, Ales, railway interlocking, ESA COMPASS/AUTOGEF, EuRailCheck, AutoFocus, Kratos, RATSY, OCRA, FSAP, and HyCOMP (Section 5, printed p. 339).

Proved or measured: the paper documents implemented algorithms, interfaces, and comparative solved-instance curves. Inference: the trusted components include the model encoding, C/C++ implementation, BDD/SAT/SMT engines, external IC3 executables, and user properties. Inference: the assumptions include synchronous transition-system semantics, faithful AIGER/SMT encodings, and sound abstraction/refinement. Inference: a Verus product could reuse the portfolio as a preprocessor for invariant candidates and counterexamples, but the paper does not establish correctness of generated Rust or of the trusted solver/compiler chain. Inference: the explicit incompleteness of infinite-state methods is a product risk when agent-written verified Rust needs a deterministic proof result.

## techniques for the catalog
- interpolation and interpolation sequences: derive invariant information from proof-producing SAT/SMT refutations
- k-induction and IC3: prove invariants with bounded induction or clause-based reachability, including abstraction-refinement variants
- SMT lifting: apply BMC, interpolation, k-induction, and IC3 to integer and real transition systems
- implicit abstraction and CEGAR: encode abstract checks in SMT, enumerate predicates with AllSMT, and refine by cores, interpolants, or weakest preconditions
- k-liveness: count fairness activations inside IC3 with an LTL monitor
- semantic model reduction: re-encode finite domains using static value analysis, constants, and free-input propagation
