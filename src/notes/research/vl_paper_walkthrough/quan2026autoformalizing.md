# Autoformalizing Argumentative Material Inferences(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)Tag: `quan2026autoformalizing`, [paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16991v1)## scope and resultThe paper studies how to make an implicit natural-language argument formally checkable when the premise does not state its warrant. Its central claim is: “We address this problem by formulating autoformalization for argumentative material inference as guard completion” (Abstract, PDF p.1). A guard is an explicit assumption, definition, or exception condition that makes a premise-to-claim relation provable. GUARD combines an LLM with Isabelle/HOL and accepts a result only after theorem-prover verification and contrastive premise and claim tests. The paper reports verified-faithful rates (VFR) of 48.58–76.52% on 247 Debatepedia pairs and 53.50–81.00% on 200 ARCT pairs, depending on backbone (Table 1, PDF p.5). These are percentages of the stated fixed sets, not success rates on arbitrary arguments.
