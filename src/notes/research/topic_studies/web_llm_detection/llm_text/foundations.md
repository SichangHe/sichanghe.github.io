what text detection can establish
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)

takeaway
- detection is a question about specified observations and specified populations
  - text alone, text plus a secret watermark key, and text plus a generation log are different observations
  - one passage and many independent passages are different experiments
- no reviewed theorem establishes that all AI text detection is impossible
  - close text distributions limit text-only detection
  - distinguishable distributions can become easier to separate with enough independent observations
  - neither statement gives reliable authorship judgments for arbitrary web pages

plain definitions
- distribution: a rule assigning probabilities to possible observations
  - human distribution H includes the people, topics, language, editing practices, and sampling procedure being studied
  - model distribution M includes the generator, prompts, settings, and later editing
- total variation distance δ
  - the largest difference between H and M in the probability of any chosen set of observations
  - δ ranges from 0 to 1
    - 0 means identical distributions
    - 1 means disjoint distributions
  - for discrete text: δ = ½ Σₓ |M(x) − H(x)|
- false-positive rate α
  - fraction of human observations wrongly flagged
- recall r
  - fraction of generated observations correctly flagged
- AUROC
  - probability that a generated observation receives a higher detector score than a human observation
    - ties count as half
  - 0.5 is chance ranking
  - 1 is perfect ranking
  - a high value does not establish useful recall at a very low false-positive rate

text-only limits
- [Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156), Sadasivan et al., arXiv 2023, 2025 revision inspected
  - publication status: preprint version reviewed
  - section 4 evidence: “any two arbitrary distributions H and M”
  - theorem 1
    - AUROC ≤ ½ + δ − δ²/2
  - appendix C.2, corollary 3
    - r ≤ α + δ
  - meaning
    - identical observed distributions give no text-only ranking advantage
    - if δ = 0.1, AUROC is at most 0.595
    - if α = 0.01 and δ = 0.1, recall is at most 0.11
  - assumptions
    - both populations use the same observation space
    - the detector acts on that observation
    - the stated H and M are the distributions actually being tested
  - no training or computation restriction is needed for theorem 1
    - even an ideal detector obeys the bound
  - scope limit
    - the theorem does not measure δ for deployed generators or real human populations
    - low δ is sufficient to limit detection
    - high δ does not guarantee that a practical detector finds the distinguishing evidence
- convergence is an additional assumption
  - section 4 evidence: “can lead to smaller TV”
    - refers to more advanced LLMs mimicking H
  - authors assume improved imitation reduces δ for a specified human distribution
  - inference: better writing quality does not itself prove smaller δ
    - a fluent generator may retain detectable metadata, formatting, or sampling patterns
    - performance on human preference tests is not total variation distance
  - finite-sample estimates over simplified features do not identify exact distance over all possible texts
- asymptotic claims require their limit to be specified
  - as δ → 0, the upper bound approaches 0.5
  - this says nothing about how quickly a particular generator approaches H
  - it does not say present-day H and M are identical
  - a finite benchmark's empirical distributions can appear disjoint because almost every full passage is unique
    - inference: this does not prove that the underlying populations are easy to distinguish
- finite random seeds introduce a separate issue
  - appendix C.4 evidence: “the bound in Theorem 1 vacuous”
    - refers to nearly disjoint human and pseudorandom output distributions
  - a finite generator seed can restrict possible outputs
    - an unlimited detector could in principle exploit the restricted support
  - theorem 2 instead restricts detectors to polynomial computation
    - assumes pseudorandom outputs cannot be efficiently distinguished from truly random sampling beyond a negligible advantage ε
    - then AUROC ≤ ½ + δ − δ²/2 + ε
  - scope limit
    - this is a computational indistinguishability assumption
    - it is not verified merely because an implementation uses some pseudorandom number generator
    - seed secrecy and the actual generator matter

many observations can change the problem
- [On the Possibilities of AI-Generated Text Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04736), Chakraborty et al., arXiv 2023
  - publication status: preprint version reviewed
  - section 3.2 evidence: “i.i.d. drawn from either the human h or machine m”
    - i.i.d. means independent observations drawn from the same distribution
  - replaces one-sample distributions by product distributions Hⁿ and Mⁿ
    - n observations all independently from H, or all independently from M
    - this is a same-origin hypothesis test, not a mixed-corpus prevalence model
  - for distinct fixed H and M, their product-distribution distance approaches 1 as n grows
  - interpretation
    - repeated independent evidence can reveal a small persistent difference
    - this does not contradict a one-observation upper bound
  - detector existence is different from implementation
    - an ideal likelihood-ratio detector needs the probability ratio M(x)/H(x)
    - those probabilities are generally unknown for web content
  - section 3.4 extends the argument to dependent samples
    - assumes independent groups with a specified dependence structure within each group
    - this does not cover arbitrary web duplication or arbitrary sentence dependence
- caution about the paper's tightness claim
  - section 3.3 evidence: “attain the bound for any distributions”
  - agent mathematical check: the AUROC upper bound need not be attained for a particular pair H and M
    - let H be a fair binary variable
    - let M assign probability 0.6 to outcome 1
    - δ = 0.1
    - optimal ranking AUROC is 0.55
      - generated outcome exceeds human outcome with probability 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.3
      - tie probability is 0.5
      - AUROC = 0.3 + 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.55
    - the stated bound is 0.595
  - the likelihood-ratio detector is optimal
    - optimality does not imply equality with every upper bound
  - recommendation: use the valid product-distribution distinction
    - verify the stronger sample-complexity derivation independently before quoting it as a guarantee
- set-level independence must be tested
  - pages from one template, translated copies, and paraphrases of one draft share evidence
  - treating them as independent exaggerates confidence
  - perfect duplication gives no additional information
    - repeating the same observation n times cannot improve its distinguishing power
  - recommendation
    - define the unit as an independently sourced document or generation session
    - group shared source, author, prompt, and website during splitting and uncertainty estimation
    - compare empirical error against naive independent-sample estimates

human populations change false-positive risk
- [GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819), Liang et al., Patterns 2023, peer reviewed
  - results evidence: “average false positive rate: 61.22%”
  - seven then-available detectors
  - 91 human TOEFL essays from a Chinese educational forum
  - 88 US eighth-grade essays from the ASAP dataset
  - 18 TOEFL essays flagged by all seven detectors
  - 89 flagged by at least one
  - editing vocabulary with ChatGPT reduced average flags from 61.22% to 11.77%
  - discussion evidence: “sample sizes employed in this research are relatively small”
  - scope limits
    - different educational levels, sources, and tasks confound a simple causal language-status interpretation
    - the results describe these populations and these detector versions
    - they do not establish the same rate for every current detector or non-native writer
  - inference
    - lexical simplicity can be mistaken for machine authorship
    - overall FPR can conceal a high subgroup FPR
    - multiple detector agreement is not independent confirmation when detectors share similar cues

retrieval asks a narrower question
- [Paraphrasing evades detectors of AI-generated text, but retrieval is an effective defense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13408), Krishna et al., NeurIPS 2023
  - appendix A.2 evidence: “Detection is specific to an API”
  - store a service's generated responses
  - compare candidate text with those stored responses
  - reported 15-million-generation experiment
    - 97.3% detection for PG19 paraphrases
    - 80.4% for Wikipedia paraphrases
    - measured at 1% human FPR
  - the observation is text plus a stored history
    - text-only distribution bounds do not automatically apply to this richer observation
    - a corresponding bound would use joint distributions over candidate text and history
  - a hit establishes similarity to a stored output
    - it does not by itself prove who wrote the submitted text
    - it does not identify the submitting person
    - it does not prove that the submitter used this API
  - appendix A.2 evidence: “False positives due to training data memorization”
    - a service can reproduce text originally written by humans
    - popular facts and common formulations can also generate similarity
  - a miss does not establish human authorship
    - another service may have generated the text
    - local models need not log outputs centrally
    - logs may be incomplete or deleted
    - editing can destroy retrieval similarity
  - recommendation
    - distinguish provider-output membership, generator attribution, and human/AI authorship in both labels and reports
    - test short queries, common topics, memorized text, and recursive paraphrases

rare generations make false flags dominate
- deduction from probability rules
  - let p be the fraction of generated observations in the tested population
  - fraction of flags that are true generations = pr / [pr + (1 − p)α]
  - this is the positive predictive value
    - it depends on prevalence p as well as detector errors
  - numerical example appears in [evaluation contract](robustness_evaluation.md)
- recommendation for web measurement
  - evaluate plausible prevalence ranges
  - do not read an uncalibrated detector score as an authorship probability
  - estimate FPR within relevant human subgroups
  - separate document decisions from population prevalence estimates
    - estimating prevalence also requires error rates representative of the population
    - drift in human style can mimic a rise in generated content

research consequences
- recommendation: study when aggregation adds independent evidence
  - compare unrelated documents, same-author documents, template siblings, and copied/paraphrased documents
  - freeze domain-specific thresholds
  - measure confidence inflation caused by correlation
- recommendation: state the observation model before claiming impossibility or possibility
  - final text alone
  - text and surrounding page
  - text and authenticated edit history
  - text and provider response database
- recommendation: claim what was measured
  - a benchmark distinguishes specified distributions
  - a detector flag supplies fallible evidence
  - authorship history requires a separate inference
