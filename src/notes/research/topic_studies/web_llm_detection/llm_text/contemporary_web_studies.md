contemporary web studies and detector validity

what already exists
- fact: two 2026 studies apply detectors to archived web pages
    - Dolezal et al. explicitly test plain text against HTML-wrapped text
    - Pew compares two Pangram detector versions on tens of thousands of pages
- inference: another prevalence estimate or a plain-text-versus-HTML comparison needs a sharper contribution
    - controlled changes to extraction and page templates remain distinguishable from their reported comparisons
    - priority: measure which pipeline choices change false positives when actual authorship is held fixed
- coverage fact: opened primary project, arXiv and Pew pages on 2026-10-06
    - read Dolezal et al.’s local PDF methods and robustness appendix
    - this note concerns measurement validity and novelty

Dolezal, Alam, Graham and Bohacek, 2026
- primary sources: [The Impact of AI-Generated Text on the Internet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26965), [project](https://ai-on-the-internet.github.io/)
    - appendix D quote: “a strict one-URL-per-host cap”
    - appendix A quote: “Identical AI-generated texts”
    - section 3.1 quote: “the longest paragraph”
    - section 3.1 quote: “only English text is retained”
    - total quoted words from the paper: 19
- sampling fact: Wayback samples cover 33 monthly intervals from August 2022 to May 2025
    - target 10,000 URLs per month
    - May 2025 ZipNum index
    - URLs first archived from January 2022 onward
    - first snapshot must return HTTP 200 and HTML
    - exclude URLs deeper than three path segments or with more than two query parameters
    - shuffle monthly buckets before sampling
- important protocol distinction: main methods describe logarithmic downsampling
    - appendix D replaces it with one URL per host
    - inference: appendix D is essential to interpreting the actual design
- limitation, inference: first archival date is not verified publication date
    - one URL per host changes weighting relative to page-uniform sampling
    - path-depth restrictions exclude part of the accessible web
    - archived successful HTML excludes unarchived or inaccessible pages
    - these restrictions prevent treating the sample as an unrestricted census of newly published pages
- extraction fact: Trafilatura removes boilerplate and extracts main text
    - split into paragraphs
    - retain the longest paragraph
    - discard pages whose selected paragraph has fewer than 100 words
    - apply langdetect and retain English
- limitation, inference: one long paragraph does not establish whole-page authorship
    - short pages and short generated passages can disappear from the analyzed population
    - main-text extraction does not establish equivalence with text actually visible after browser execution
- detector-selection fact: compare Binoculars, Desklib, DivEye and commercial Pangram v3
    - text length, HTML wrapping, generator family, generator version and language
    - use Pangram v3 for main analysis
    - model returns fractions labeled human, machine and assisted
- HTML experiment fact: identical GPT-4o text supplied as plain text and embedded HTML
    - author-reported Binoculars mean score changes from 0.745 to 0.823
    - flag rate falls from 100% to 88.6%
    - Pangram distributions overlap in the reported comparison
- limitation, inference: this experiment evaluates representation sensitivity directly
    - it does not separately isolate extraction mistakes, template content, paragraph selection and authorship mixing
    - GPT-4o outputs alone do not measure human false positives under HTML changes
    - a positive-only flag-rate comparison cannot establish deployment specificity
- robustness evidence fact: length test uses 20 human and 20 machine examples per length band
    - inference: apparent perfect accuracy in such a small band is weak evidence for a very low population false-positive rate
    - more than 100-word selection reduces short-text problems but also changes the target population
- calibration uncertainty: section 3.3 refers to a cutoff for classifying fully machine-generated pages
    - exact operational cutoff was not identified in the inspected methods and appendix passages
    - recommendation: inspect released code before attempting exact reproduction
- validity implication, inference: a detector’s assisted fraction is a model prediction
    - it is not an observed record of the author’s editing history
    - mapping that score to actual drafting/editing workflows needs independent labels

Pew Research Center, 2026-08-20
- primary source: [methodology for AI content measurement](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/08/20/methodology-ai-content/)
    - calibration footnote quote: “closely calibrate the results from Open Pangram to those from Pangram 3.3”
    - dated-subset quote: “not a random subset of the web”
    - total quoted words from this source: 24
- sampling fact: 10,000 English pages from each of 49 Common Crawl snapshots
    - January 2021 through July 2026
    - 490,000 pages total
    - public pages required
    - paywalled and login-restricted content underrepresented
- representation fact: collect both WARC and WET records
    - WARC contains archived full HTML
    - WET contains extracted page text without HTML and other media
    - detector input is the extracted text
- detector fact: editlens_Llama-3.2-3B from Pangram
    - numeric score between zero and one
    - flag meaningful authorship/editing signs at score at least 0.2
- calibration fact: choose 0.2 to align open-model labels with commercial Pangram 3.3 categories
    - inference: commercial agreement is not calibration against independently observed authorship
    - correlated detector errors can survive this alignment
- agreement fact: compare 62,370 pages from seven crawls
    - 96% agreement
    - Cohen’s kappa 0.61
    - kappa measures agreement after accounting for expected agreement from label frequencies
    - inference: high raw agreement alone can hide disagreement in a rare positive class
- false-positive evidence: open model flags approximately 1% in early pre-ChatGPT crawls
    - commercial model flags much less
    - authors caution that early reported shares likely include misclassified human content
    - inference: pre-ChatGPT pages provide a useful control, but old page style differs from contemporary human writing
- date limitation: publication-date fields found on about 10–15% of sampled pages
    - post-ChatGPT analysis uses that dated subset
    - authors explicitly restrict its interpretation to dated content
    - inference: undated pages can have systematically different topics, templates and authorship
- inference: consistent use of one detector helps compare time points only if its errors remain sufficiently stable
    - changing page styles, text lengths and editing practices can change false positives and misses
    - agreement with a second related model does not directly estimate these changes

concrete novelty distinction
- already done: feed identical generated text directly as plain text and as HTML
    - measure detector score changes
- proposed extension: hold article authorship and wording fixed
    - intervene on navigation, ads, footer, template, embedded quotations and paragraph boundaries
    - run multiple extractors on every page version
    - compare extracted passages with an independently labeled article span
    - classify raw HTML, extracted main text, longest paragraph and browser-visible text separately
- contribution sought: distinguish three causes of changed detection
    - detector reacts to representation despite unchanged content
    - extraction adds or deletes article words
    - included template text has a different author from the article
- evaluation recommendation: include known human and machine articles
    - repeated templates crossed with both classes
    - hold out whole template families and websites
    - freeze detector and cutoff before test interventions
    - report both human false positives and machine detection rate
- inference: this establishes pipeline effects more directly than comparing aggregate scores across uncontrolled pages
    - novelty is provisional until source code and additional extraction studies are checked

reproduction checks before a larger study
- recommendation: reconstruct Dolezal et al.’s implemented sample and cutoff from released code
    - resolve main-text versus appendix sampling descriptions
    - record exclusions after extraction and language filtering
- recommendation: reproduce Pew’s open/commercial alignment
    - add an independent labeled calibration set
    - compare 0.2 with cutoffs targeting specific human false-positive rates
- recommendation: measure sampled-page retention by date, domain and template
    - report selection effects separately from detector errors
- recommendation: reserve independent contemporary human writing for low false-positive validation
    - pre-ChatGPT material alone does not validate contemporary prose
