Caveats on method:
- WebFetch passes pages through a small summarizer. Text in quotation marks is the tool's rendering and is usually verbatim. The "verbatim" status of each quote was not independently checked against the page, so spot-check before citing.
- I did not read full PDFs. Quotes are from abstracts, landing pages and blog pages. "Where in source" is mostly not visible.
- Several arXiv IDs fetched are in the 26xx range, with dates in July to September 2026 (today is 7 Oct 2026). I report them as the fetched pages gave them.
- Items marked [NOT FETCHED] are named only because they appeared in search results or from memory. Treat them as leads.

## A. Empirical studies of Rust concurrency and safety

1. **Understanding Memory and Thread Safety Practices and Issues in Real-World Rust Programs**
   - Authors: Boqin Qin, Yilun Chen, Zeming Yu, Linhai Song, Yiying Zhang. PLDI 2020.
   - URL: https://pldi20.sigplan.org/details/pldi-2020-papers/76/Understanding-Memory-and-Thread-Safety-Practices-and-Issues-in-Real-World-Rust-Progra
   - Per the search summary of the abstract: manual inspection of "850 unsafe code usages and 170 bugs", across five projects, five libraries, two security databases and the standard library.
   - Findings summarized by the tool: all memory-safety bugs involve unsafe code. Two static detectors were built (the lockbud lineage).
   - Claim: thread-safety bugs are mostly lock and channel misuse.
   - Gap: the study predates mainstream async (2020), so futures and cancellation are not categories.
   - [Quotes were not retrieved verbatim.]

2. **Fearless Concurrency? Understanding Concurrent Programming Safety in Real-World Rust Software**
   - Authors: Zeming Yu, Linhai Song, Yiying Zhang. arXiv 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01906.
   - Quote (abstract): "there is limited understanding of how the checking rules influence the safety of concurrent programming in Rust applications."
   - Search summary: "double locks and misusing channels are two common causes of deadlocks in Rust".
   - Inference: the same predate-async gap as item 1.

3. **Understanding and Detecting Real-World Safety Issues in Rust**
   - Authors: Qin, Chen, Liu, Zhang, Wen, Song, Zhang. IEEE TSE 2024, DOI 10.1109/TSE.2024.3380393.
   - URL: https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10509785 (PDF fetch failed, so metadata only).
   - Search-summary claim: the study covers memory safety, thread safety (deadlocks and races) and panics. It is cited by lockbud as its tool paper.
   - Inference: this is the most direct sync-Rust baseline. Whether it handles `async` is unverified.

4. **Memory-Safety Challenge Considered Solved? An In-Depth Study with All Rust CVEs** (title from memory)
   - arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03296.
   - Quote (summarized): "all memory-safety bugs require unsafe code". The study covers 186 reports through December 2020.
   - Relevance: low for async. It gives a baseline.

5. **Rudra: Finding Memory Safety Bugs in Rust at the Ecosystem Scale**
   - Authors: Yechan Bae, Youngsuk Kim, Ammar Askar, Jungwon Lim, Taesoo Kim. SOSP 2021.
   - URL: https://acawiki.org/Rudra:_Finding_Memory_Safety_Bugs_in_Rust_at_the_Ecosystem_Scale
   - Claims (search summary): scanned 43k packages in 6.5 hours. It found 264 bugs, 76 CVEs and 112 RustSec advisories. Three patterns: panic safety, higher-order invariant, and Send/Sync variance.
   - Relevance: Send/Sync variance is the only async-adjacent thread-safety pattern in these studies. A Send/Sync bug can let a future cross threads unsoundly.

6. **UnsafeChecker: Finding Soundness Bugs in Rust Safe Abstractions**
   - Authors: Xizhe Yin, Yaokun Zhang, Yang Feng, Baowen Xu. arXiv 2609.09641, 9 Sep 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09641
   - Results: 114 new bugs in 83 crates, 45 confirmed. It reaches 67.9% recall on a 46-vulnerability RustSec benchmark.
   - Fetched summary: "does not explicitly mention Send/Sync".
   - Gap: no async or concurrency soundness focus.

7. **An Empirical Study of Rust-Specific Bugs in the rustc Compiler**
   - arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23985, 2025. 301 issues from 2022 to 2024.
   - Relevance: compiler bugs only. I did not check whether async bugs appear. It does not answer the async-bug question.
   - Also from search results [NOT FETCHED]: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28713 (rustc type-checker soundness bugs).

## B. Practitioner and primary-source accounts of async bugs

8. **RFD 400, "Dealing with cancel safety in async Rust"**
   - Author: Rain Paharia (Oxide). Oxide RFD site, https://rfd.shared.oxide.computer/rfd/0400
   - Quote: "a future is _cancel-safe_ if it can be dropped before being completed, without any implications for the rest of the system."
   - Quote: `select!` is "perhaps the most common source of async cancellation bugs."
   - Quote: `Sender::send` loses values because "the value will be dropped—lost in the ether".
   - Bugs reported: Propolis serial console proxy data loss, wicketd progress tracker with invalid state across awaits (tokio mutexes), sled-agent zone deletion cancelled early.
   - Claim (author): cancel correctness is a global property of the system.
   - Inference: this is the best bug-catalog source I found. It is a few bugs from one company, not a corpus.

9. **Cancelling async Rust**
   - Author: Rain (sunshowers). 3 Oct 2025, updated 24 Oct. https://sunshowers.io/posts/cancelling-async-rust/
   - Quote: select loops "are particularly prone to cancel correctness issues". Cancellation is "probably the least Rusty part of Rust".
   - Claim: a bug needs three conditions: a cancel-unsafe future, cancellation, and a violated property.
   - Production bug: invariants set to `None`, then not restored when cancelled at an await inside a tokio-mutex critical section.
   - The page's phrasing is paraphrased by the tool, so check these quotes.

10. **Canceling asynchronous Rust (LWN)**
    - Author: Daroc Alden, on Paharia's RustConf 2025 talk. 24 Sep 2025. https://lwn.net/Articles/1036924/
    - Mitigations: `.reserve()`-style splitting, progress-tracking APIs, spawning threads or tasks.
    - Quote: "Async drop, which would let asynchronous code run a cleanup function on cancellation". The other two proposals are unforgettable types and undroppable types.

11. **RFD 609, "Futurelock"**
    - Author: David Pacheco (Oxide). https://rfd.shared.oxide.computer/rfd/0609 (2025; the page date was not visible).
    - Definition: "a type of deadlock where a resource owned by Future A is required for another Future B to proceed, while the Task responsible for both Futures is no longer polling A."
    - Scenarios: `select!` on `&mut future`, FuturesUnordered/Ordered, custom futures.
    - Mitigation: "spawn the future in a new task."
    - Inference: a bug class that Send/Sync, borrowck and `await_holding_lock` do not catch. It is a strong target for a lint or detector.

12. **How to deadlock a Tokio application with just a single mutex**
    - Author: Piotr Jastrzebski (Turso). 2 Sep 2024. https://turso.tech/blog/how-to-deadlock-tokio-application-in-rust-with-just-a-single-mutex
    - Quote: "Asynchronous task just obtains the lock and then releases it immediately but synchronous task obtains the lock and then blocks on a future while keeping this lock."
    - Mechanism: `block_on` (or `block_in_place`) while holding a `std` mutex, plus worker starvation.
    - The article's own remedy was `tokio::sync::Mutex`. That does not remove the block-on-while-holding pattern, and I did not verify it end to end.

13. **Cancelling Your Future in Tokio**
    - Authors: Samyak Sarnayak and Amit Mandal (e6data). Rust India Conference 2026, April 2026. https://hasgeek.com/rustbangalore/rust-india-conference-2026/sub/cancelling-your-future-in-tokio-cancellation-in-as-4AwvaboSMRAqKqEXJxTkQu
    - Quote: "The compiler isn't as helpful in async rust, so devs need to exercise more caution."
    - Two production bugs in a DataFusion-based OLAP engine: a task that keeps running after cancel, and a deadlock with one unlocked tokio mutex.
    - This is the only DataFusion-related async-bug source found. Abstract only.

14. **Rust async traps**
    - Author: qouteall. 2 Oct 2026. https://qouteall.fun/qouteall-blog/2026/Rust async traps
    - Quote: "When you don't want cancellation, then the implicit cancellation is a trap. It causes bugs and leave no log by default."
    - Quote: "The un-`poll`-ed future holds lock. Its held lock will only release in two cases: 1. the future is polled and progresses, 2. the future is dropped."
    - Also covers blocking the scheduler thread, unbounded concurrency, and io_uring buffer safety after cancel (a kernel write into a freed buffer).
    - Blog, not research.

15. **The Evolution of Async Rust: From Tokio to High-Level Applications**
    - Author: Tatiana Parshutkina; interview with Carl Lerche. JetBrains blog, 17 Feb 2026. https://blog.jetbrains.com/rust/2026/02/17/the-evolution-of-async-rust-from-tokio-to-high-level-applications/
    - Quote: "Because async is cooperative scheduling, you have to make sure you're yielding back to the runtime regularly enough."
    - Claim: debugging stuck tasks and tail latency is "particularly difficult".

## C. Language design

16. **A Design Space Exploration of Async/Await**
    - Authors: Gavin Gray, Shriram Krishnamurthi, Will Crichton. arXiv 2608.20677, 21 Aug 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20677
    - Quote: "no two of them agree as a whole on design decisions that affect the presence and ordering of execution".
    - Nine design dimensions, including task completion and cancellation. The search snippet says Tokio and Smol implement "unaware cancellation".
    - Inference: it gives a vocabulary for comparing Rust's drop-based cancellation, but I did not read it in full.

17. **Rust Async Goals / Roadmap: "Just add async" (2026)**
    - Contact: Niko Matsakis. Task owners include Benno Lossin, TC, lcnr, Yoshua Wuyts, Santiago Pastorino. https://goals.rust-lang.org/2026/roadmap-just-add-async.html
    - Quote: "Patterns that work in sync Rust should work in async Rust without requiring workarounds, restructuring, or arcane incantations."
    - Quote: "Sync Rust has `std::thread::scope` for borrowing into spawned threads. Async spawn APIs require `'static`, forcing `Arc` everywhere."
    - Quote: "Resources that need async cleanup (database connections, network sessions) can't clean up properly in `Drop`."
    - Claim: streams "interact with structured concurrency and cancellation", and async iteration is not a 2026 focus.
    - Inference: the project's 2026 emphasis is async fn in traits and closures, not cancellation bugs.

18. **Rust 2025H1 Project Goals, async parity**
    - Contact: Tyler Mandry. https://goals.rust-lang.org/2025h1/index.html
    - The goal "bring the Async Rust experience closer to parity with sync Rust". It lists async fn in traits, Pin ergonomics and generators.
    - An async-drop prototype goal is mentioned only via search snippets: an "experiment aimed at uncovering implementation hurdles", needing components sufficient for "a working and sound library for scoped tasks and/or structured concurrency".

19. **Guaranteeing async drop (async-fundamentals-initiative)**
    - https://rust-lang.github.io/async-fundamentals-initiative/evaluation/challenges/guaranteeing_async_drop.html
    - Quote: "For any type `MyStruct` that implements `AsyncDrop`, it is always possible in Rust today to drop an instance of `MyStruct` in synchronous code."
    - Options listed: abort/panic, forced blocking, or a language extension. A lint is suggested as a mitigation.

20. **poll_progress** (boats)
    - without.boats, 12 Dec 2023. https://without.boats/blog/poll-progress/
    - Problem: "Barbara battles buffered streams", where the buffered child futures stall while the `for await` body awaits.
    - Fix proposed: a `poll_progress` method that "allows it to make progress without calling `poll_next`".
    - Relevance: this is the same family of bug as futurelock (item 11). It is older than the 2024 to 2026 window.

## D. Detection and verification tools

21. **lockbud** (BurtonQin)
    - https://github.com/BurtonQin/lockbud
    - Detects double-lock, conflicting-lock-order, condvar misuse, atomicity violations, use-after-free, invalid-free, and panic locations.
    - Quote: "The deadlock detectors (double-lock and conflicting-lock-order) perform better than the other detectors, as the project was initially designed for deadlock detection."
    - It is not described as await-aware. The README's only async mention is blacklisting `tokio_util` to cut false positives.
    - Inference: it does not model task or await wait-for edges (futurelock, `block_on` under a lock).

22. **Static Deadlock Detection for Rust Programs (RcChecker)**
    - Authors: Yu Zhang, Kaiwen Zhang, Guanjun Liu (Tongji). arXiv 2401.01114, v1 Jan 2024, v2 Jan 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01114
    - Quote (abstract): "our method outperforms existing deadlock detection methods in precision."
    - Search summary: it removes 8 resource-deadlock false positives and finds 17 errors lockbud misses. It also reports 7 new issues (2 resource, 5 communication deadlocks). The tool uses a signal-lock graph.
    - It covers threads, locks and condvars. I did not check for futures or async.

23. **Petri Nets-based Methods for Detecting Concurrency Bugs in Rust Programs**
    - Authors: Kaiwen Zhang, Guanjun Liu. arXiv 2212.02754, v3 23 Sep 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02754
    - Claim: "35.7% fewer false positives and 28.3% fewer false negatives" than LockBud, and "100% faster verification speed" than Miri.
    - The search summary said LockBud has false positives and negatives due to inadequate path-sensitive alias analysis.

24. **Miri**
    - https://github.com/rust-lang/miri. It detects data races with vector clocks and emulates some weak-memory effects.
    - From the search result, Miri does not support tokio, per Miri's list of unsupported tokio features. I did not read Miri's README directly.
    - Inference: Miri cannot be applied directly to typical async programs.

25. **loom** (tokio-rs)
    - https://github.com/tokio-rs/loom. It "runs a test many times, permuting the possible concurrent executions of that test under the C11 memory model".
    - Limits quoted: `SeqCst` is treated as acquire-release, so "Loom may generate false alarms". It does not explore all C11 executions, such as load buffering, so it can miss bugs.
    - Inference: it models threads and atomics. Whether it models task cancellation is not stated.

26. **Kani**
    - Docs: https://model-checking.github.io/kani/rust-feature-support.html. Paper: Delmas et al., "Kani: A Model Checker for Rust", arXiv 2607.01504, July 2026 (also ASE 2026 industry showcase).
    - Docs quote: "Concurrent features are currently out of scope for Kani." Await expressions and data races are listed as unsupported. Concurrent code is compiled as if sequential.
    - Docs quote: "verification of concurrent programs continues to be an open research problem".
    - Paper: finds 6 previously unknown bugs in industrial projects and runs 16,000+ harnesses per change. The abstract fetched says nothing on concurrency.
    - Search snippet: the backends (CBMC and ESBMC) were designed for concurrency, so the limitation is in the frontend.

27. **Clippy lints**
    - Docs: https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-clippy/master/index.html#await_holding_lock
    - `await_holding_lock` flags `await` while holding a non-async-aware guard. Quote: std and parking_lot mutexes "are not designed to operate in an async context across await points."
    - `await_holding_refcell_ref` does the same for RefCell. `await_holding_invalid_type` is configurable.
    - Inference: these are intra-procedural and syntactic. They do not catch cancellation safety or futurelock.

28. **tokio-console**
    - https://github.com/tokio-rs/console
    - Warns on `lost-waker` ("Warns when a task is dropped without being woken"), `self-wakes`, `never-yielded` and `large-future`.
    - Needs `tokio_unstable`.
    - Inference: it is a dynamic diagnostic, not a bug-finder for cancellation or futurelock.

29. **Leaf: An Instrumentation-based Dynamic Analysis Framework for Rust**
    - Authors: Tehrani, Gaboardi, Sumner, Ko. arXiv 2607.15025, July 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15025
    - It captures MIR-level semantics at runtime. Its three analyses are a concolic executor, a sanitizer and a control-flow tracer.
    - Relevance: whether it handles async was not confirmed. It is a possible basis for an async dynamic tool.

## E. Open problems and gaps, each tied to a source

- **No systematic async-bug corpus.** Searches found only anecdotes: items 8, 9, 12, 13 and 14. Qin et al. (1, 3) and Yu et al. (2) predate async. A mined study of tokio-based projects, with cancel-safety and select! classes, appears unpublished.
- **Cancel-safety detection is open.** Item 8 defines the property only informally, and item 10 lists mitigations that rely on programmer discipline. A checker would need annotations or inference of cancel-unsafe futures, the select! sites that can cancel them, and the state invariants broken at await points.
- **Futurelock is undetected by tools.** Items 11 and 14 describe it. Items 21 to 23 and 27 target locks, not await-based wait-for graphs. A lint or detector for a `&mut` future held in a select! alongside another awaited future seems new.
- **Verification of async Rust.** Kani explicitly lacks await support (26). Miri lacks tokio support (24). loom's models are thread-oriented (25). An executor-aware model checker for tokio tasks is open.
- **Async drop gives no guarantee** (19). Cleanup can be skipped by synchronous drop. This ties to cancellation bugs where cleanup is skipped (13) and to io_uring buffer safety (14).
- **Scoped tasks are blocked by `'static` spawn bounds** (17). This pushes `Arc` and shared state everywhere, which in turn creates the lock and cancellation issues above. Evaluating the bug impact is open.
- **Distributed Rust systems.** No bug study found for TiKV, Databend, RisingWave, Materialize, Neon, Quickwit, Fluvio or Ballista. Only the e6data/DataFusion talk (13) touches this area. A study could join GitHub issues and postmortems to the async classes above.
- **Cross-language comparison.** Gray et al. (16) give a design-space vocabulary but not bug rates. Comparing cancellation-bug rates across languages is open.
