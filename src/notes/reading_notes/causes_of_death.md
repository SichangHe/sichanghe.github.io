# How humans die or become disabled, and how to avoid it

(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)

Population evidence synthesis from major journals and official statistics. Not personal medical advice. Written 2026-11-10 from the agent's knowledge; figures approximate. Companion to [longevity takeaways](longevity.md).

## Bottom line

- Most deaths in rich countries come from a few slow chronic diseases: cardiovascular disease, cancer, chronic lung disease, dementia, diabetes/kidney disease.
- A few modifiable risk factors drive most of them: high blood pressure, smoking, high glucose, high LDL/ApoB, obesity, air pollution, alcohol (GBD 2021 risk factors, Lancet 2024).
- Before ~45, the main killers differ: accidents (drug overdose, vehicles), suicide, homicide (CDC).

## What kills people

- global (GBD 2021, Lancet 2024): ischemic heart disease, stroke, COPD, lower respiratory infection, COVID-19 (2020–21), lung cancer, dementia, diabetes, kidney disease
- US (CDC 2023): heart disease, cancer, unintentional injury, stroke, chronic lower respiratory disease, Alzheimer's, diabetes, kidney disease, liver disease
- cancer deaths: lung by far the largest, then colorectal, pancreatic, breast, prostate (ACS, CA Cancer J Clin)

## Interventions with strong (trial or overwhelming) evidence

- stop smoking
    - smokers lose ~10 years; quitting before 40 recovers ~90% of the loss (Jha 2013, NEJM; Doll 2004, BMJ)
- lower blood pressure
    - intensive target (systolic <120) cut cardiovascular events and death vs <140 in high-risk adults (SPRINT 2015, NEJM)
- lower LDL/ApoB
    - each 1 mmol/L LDL reduction cuts major vascular events ~20% (CTT, Lancet 2010); see [cholesterol note](cholesterol_cvd.md)
- control glucose and weight
    - GLP-1 drug semaglutide cut cardiovascular events 20% in obese non-diabetics (SELECT 2023, NEJM)
- cancer screening
    - lung CT for heavy smokers cut lung-cancer death 20% (NLST 2011, NEJM)
    - colonoscopy invitation: ~18% lower colorectal-cancer incidence, mortality effect not significant at 10 years (NordICC 2022, NEJM)
- vaccines
    - HPV vaccine before 17 cut invasive cervical cancer ~88% (Lei 2020, NEJM); influenza/COVID/pneumococcal vaccines reduce respiratory deaths in the elderly
- dementia
    - 14 modifiable risk factors (hearing loss, LDL, hypertension, smoking, inactivity, etc.) account for ~45% of cases, a modeling estimate (Lancet Commission 2024)

## Strong but observational

- exercise and cardiorespiratory fitness: among the strongest mortality predictors; causal size unclear
- sleep, diet quality, social connection: consistent associations, weaker causal evidence
- moderate drinking "benefit" mostly vanishes after correcting for sick ex-drinkers; less alcohol is safer

## Disabling outcomes besides death

- top causes of years lived with disability (GBD 2021, Lancet 2024): low back pain, depression, headache, falls, diabetes, hearing loss, vision loss, dementia, stroke
- blindness/vision loss
    - main causes: cataract and uncorrected refractive error (both fixable), glaucoma, age-related macular degeneration (AMD), diabetic retinopathy (Lancet Global Health Commission on Global Eye Health 2021)
    - prevention: regular eye exams after 40 (glaucoma is silent; drops/laser stop progression), glucose and blood-pressure control (diabetic retinopathy), not smoking (AMD), AREDS2 supplement only for intermediate AMD (AREDS2 2013, JAMA), anti-VEGF injections treat wet AMD
    - myopia: more outdoor time in childhood lowers onset (He 2015, JAMA); high myopia raises retinal detachment and glaucoma risk
- stroke disability: same levers as stroke death, mainly blood pressure; atrial fibrillation needs anticoagulation
- hearing loss: avoid loud noise, use hearing aids; hearing aids slowed cognitive decline in at-risk older adults (ACHIEVE 2023, Lancet)
- falls/fractures in the elderly: strength and balance exercise reduce falls (Cochrane 2019); treat osteoporosis

## Actionable (inference, for a healthy adult)

- don't smoke; drive sober, belted, undistracted; avoid opioids outside supervision
- know and treat blood pressure, ApoB/LDL, HbA1c early
- exercise regularly, including cardio fitness and strength
- get age-appropriate screening (colorectal from 45 in US) and vaccines
- protect hearing; treat hearing loss (dementia risk)
