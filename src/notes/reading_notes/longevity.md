# Longevity research takeaways

(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)

Population/biology evidence synthesis, top-tier venues only. Not personal medical advice. Written 2026-11-10 from the agent's knowledge of the literature; PDFs and DOIs in `/hdd1/sichanghe/paper_collection/longevity/index.md`.

## Bottom line

- Lifespan in animals is clearly modifiable: calorie restriction (CR), rapamycin, and removing senescent cells extend mouse lifespan in well-controlled studies.
- No intervention is yet proven to extend human lifespan or healthspan.
- David Sinclair's program (sirtuins → NAD+ → epigenetic information loss → reprogramming) produced high-profile papers, but each early pillar was weakened by later independent work; the newest pillar (reprogramming) is promising but early.

## Established (replicated, independent labs)

- Aging has a set of interacting mechanisms ("hallmarks"): genomic instability, telomere loss, epigenetic change, proteostasis loss, nutrient-sensing dysregulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, senescence, stem-cell exhaustion, altered communication, plus inflammation, dysbiosis, impaired autophagy (López-Otín 2013 and 2023, Cell).
- Rapamycin (mTOR inhibitor) extends mouse lifespan even when started late; replicated across sites by the NIA Interventions Testing Program (ITP) (Harrison 2009, Nature).
- Clearing p16Ink4a-positive senescent cells extends median lifespan and delays age-related organ deterioration in mice (Baker 2016, Nature).
- CR improves health in rhesus monkeys; survival improved in the Wisconsin study but not significantly in the NIA study, a gap Mattison 2017 (Nat Commun) attributes partly to diet and start age without fully resolving it. In humans, 2-year moderate CR improves cardiometabolic risk factors, not proven outcomes (CALERIE 2, Kraus 2019, Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol).
- DNA-methylation "clocks" predict age and mortality risk; DunedinPACE measures rate of aging (Belsky 2022, eLife). Inference: clocks are biomarkers, not proof that changing a clock changes lifespan.

## Contested

- Sirtuins / resveratrol
    - claim: resveratrol activates SIRT1 and extends yeast lifespan (Howitz 2003, Nature) and mice on high-calorie diet (Baur 2006, Nature)
    - critique: activation was an artifact of fluorescent test substrates (Pacholec 2010, J Biol Chem); Sir2 overexpression lifespan effects in worms and flies vanished after controlling genetic background (Burnett 2011, Nature)
    - rebuttal: SIRT1 allosteric activation is real for some substrates (Hubbard 2013, Science)
    - decisive: resveratrol did not extend lifespan of normal-diet mice in the ITP (Strong 2013, J Gerontol A)
    - read: no reliable lifespan benefit in healthy mammals
- NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR)
    - claim: NAD+ falls with age; restoring it reverses mitochondrial decline in mouse muscle (Gomes 2013, Cell) and mitigates age-related metabolic, activity, and eye-function decline (Mills 2016, Cell Metab)
    - human: NMN raised muscle insulin sensitivity in prediabetic women, a modest single-endpoint effect (Yoshino 2021, Science)
    - against: NR did not extend lifespan in the ITP (Harrison 2021, Aging Cell)
    - read: raises NAD+ reliably; healthspan/lifespan benefit in humans unshown
    - regulatory side-story: FDA in 2022 ruled NMN excluded from supplements because it was under drug investigation, then on 2025-09-29 said NMN is lawful in supplements because it was sold as one before the drug investigation (FDA response to Natural Products Association, reported by [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/12/09/fda-reinstates-ndi-status-of-nmn-with-new-letters-to-ingredient-players/)); legal status says nothing about efficacy
- Information theory of aging (Sinclair)
    - claim: loss of epigenetic information causes aging; inducing DNA breaks (ICE mice) accelerates aging, and OSK reprogramming reverses it (Yang 2023, Cell)
    - critique (inference/opinion from field): ICE damage may mimic damage rather than normal aging; the "cause" claim outruns the evidence
- Partial reprogramming
    - cyclic OSKM expression improved hallmarks and lifespan in progeroid mice (Ocampo 2016, Cell)
    - OSK gene therapy restored vision in old and glaucomatous mice (Lu 2020, Nature)
    - risks: teratoma and loss of cell identity; effect on normal-mouse lifespan is small or unclear
    - read: most promising rejuvenation idea; years from human proof

## Actionable (inference, for a healthy adult)

- Proven levers remain boring: exercise, not smoking, blood pressure/ApoB/glucose control, sleep, avoiding obesity (see [cholesterol note](cholesterol_cvd.md)).
- Do not expect supplements (resveratrol, NMN, NR) to extend life; top-tier evidence does not support it.
- Rapamycin, metformin (TAME trial), and senolytics are research-stage for longevity in humans.
- Treat epigenetic-clock "age reversal" claims as biomarker changes, not outcome evidence.
